# Physical pieces

- **Only the carrier sees a piece while it's being carried.** Others see the move once it's made, animated from the piece's square.
- **32 piece objects per board**, one per piece. A side never has more than 16 pieces, since promotion turns a pawn into another piece rather than adding one. Each object swaps its mesh to match the piece the board says it is, so promotion needs no spare queens. Captured pieces stand in their places beside the board (step 12.4), in the order they were captured.
- **Positions come from the board, on every client.** After every `OnBoardChanged()`, every client puts each piece on the square the board says. Nothing about the pieces is synced; the board's synced fields are the single source of truth.
- **No `VRCObjectSync`** (decided in 9.6). It was planned for carrying, but it moves a piece on every other client to where its owner put it, over that client's own placement. Clients don't agree on which object stands for which piece (a late joiner builds its mapping from scratch, while others' mappings follow their history), so with it a late joiner saw objects on the owner's squares wearing its own meshes: pieces in the wrong places. Without it, which object is which doesn't matter, as each client swaps the meshes itself.
- **Grabbing is gated by the board.** Grabbing a piece takes ownership of the *piece*, which the board's ownership rules don't cover. So the view sets each piece's `VRCPickup.pickupable` for the local player from a board getter ("may the local player touch pieces?"), updated on every change. Bystanders can't grab pieces during a game, but anyone can on a reset board. The pieces' `OnOwnershipRequest` asks the board too, as a second check. Only VR players get pickups; desktop and mobile players select and place pieces by clicking (see [Desktop mode](player-input.md#desktop-mode)).
- **Mapping:** the view keeps an array from square to piece object, rebuilt from board state. A move updates it; a reset or load rebuilds it.

## Implementation

`ChessBoardView` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessBoardView.cs`, step 9.1) is a `ChessBoardListener` holding no game state. Its local space is the board's: the playing surface at y = 0, files a–h along +x, ranks 1–8 along +z (White at −z), the centre at the origin, `squareSize` (0.05) per square.

- **Objects:** `pieces`, `pieceFilters` and `pieceRenderers`, 32 each: 0–15 White's, 16–31 Black's, so an object's colour is fixed by its index and only its model changes, from the piece set its side wears (steps 12.3, 12.10 and 13.6, below): its template's mesh, materials, turn and size for the piece it stands for.
- **Mapping:** `squareObject[64]` (`NoObject` = −1) and `objectSquare[32]` (`NoSquare` for an object off the board), with `objectType[32]` the type each object stands for.
- **A move one ply on** from the position the mapping shows (`ChangeMove` outside replay, or `ChangeReplayStep` forward by one) is applied to the mapping inside `OnBoardChanged`, so the object that moved is the one that was on its from square: the mover to its square (a promotion changes its type), a capture (en passant included) off the board, castling's king and rook to g/c and f/d, both leaving before either lands (Chess960).
- **Everything else rebuilds** from the board: objects standing on a square that now holds something else leave it, then each square without its object takes a free one of its colour, preferring one already of the piece's type, then the one that stood nearest. A position with more than 16 pieces of a colour (only a hand-made one) leaves the extra ones unshown, with a warning.
- **Placing** happens on the frame after a change (`_Refresh`, public for tests), like the debug view's drawing, so a test making thousands of changes in a frame stays within Udon's time limit. Objects off the board stand in their captured places, or are hidden (step 12.4, below).
- **Builder:** **Tools > Chess Board > Create Board View** (`Editor/ChessBoardViewBuilder.cs`) places a view for the selected board (or the scene's only one), replacing an earlier view in place: since step 14.3 an instance of the **Chess Board View** prefab ([Prefabs](prefabs.md#chess-board-view)), which it makes when missing (`BuildView`: a table, the board, the squares and the 32 pieces, with no board), its `board` set on the instance; **Update View Prefab** saves the prefab again and makes the scene's views built before 14.3 instances of it. The UI beside it is likewise an instance of the **Chess Board UI** prefab since step 14.4 ([Prefabs](prefabs.md#chess-board-ui); `BuildUI`, **Update UI Prefab**), its `board` set on the instance (and its `historyWall` before 13.10). The piece meshes and materials are generated placeholders in `Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/Models/`, made only when missing (origin at the piece's base centre, knights facing +z); the default piece set made from them, **Chess Pieces**, is a prefab nested in the Chess Arbiter prefab (steps 12.3, 13.6 and 14.1). Rebuilding keeps a world creator's own model and the model parts they deleted (step 12.2).

### Board model and scale (step 12.2)

- **Nothing reads the model:** the view never touches **Table**, **Board** or **Squares**, so a world creator can delete them and put their own model in their place (see [Customizing](customizing.md)). The square targets, which take the clicks, are separate from the model.
- **Local space:** everything is placed in the view's local space (pieces, captured pieces, highlights, the promotion ghosts, animations), and drops are read through the view's transform (`_DropSquare`), so the view is scaled through its transform, uniformly. `squareSize` stays the builder's (the square targets and highlight meshes are built for it). The groups holding the view's objects must stay at the view's origin, unturned and unscaled: `LocalSpaceProblem()` names the first object whose group isn't, and `Start` logs it as a warning.
- **Rebuilding:** **Create Board View** leaves out of the new view a **Table**, **Board** or **Squares** the old one lacked, and moves the old view's children it didn't make (a creator's model) to the new one, keeping their local place; the log says what it kept.
- Decided in 12.2: scaling is the transform's, not `squareSize`'s, since the transform already scales the pieces, colliders, targets and highlights together, while a changed `squareSize` would need every one of them rebuilt to match.

### Piece sets (steps 12.3, 12.10, 12.11 and 13.6)

- **`ChessPieceSet`** (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessPieceSet.cs`, no synced variables) holds both colours' pieces again (step 13.6, changed from 12.10's one side per set): White's 6 templates (`whitePawn` … `whiteKing`) and 4 promotion templates (step 12.11: `whitePromotionKnight` … `whitePromotionQueen`, for the promotion ghosts), and Black's the same (`blackPawn` …, `blackPromotion…`), read by `_Template(color, type)` and `_PromotionTemplate(color, type)`. Each is an object with the model's `MeshFilter` and `MeshRenderer` and its materials, every slot. A template's local position, rotation and scale (under its parent, which stands for the square's centre on the playing surface) are the piece's offset, turn and size on its square, in the view's local units: White's set up facing Black (+z), Black's facing White (−z), so the view turns neither. On Start the set deactivates its children (the templates and guides), which the views go on reading. White's templates are all required: `Problem()` names a missing White template (a promotion one too), mesh or renderer; Black's are optional.
- **Black's fallback** (step 13.6): a Black template with no mesh (missing, or its `MeshFilter` empty or absent; `ChessPieceSet._HasMesh`) wears White's mesh and transform for that type, turned half round about the vertical through the square's centre (as the view turned every Black piece before 13.6, so the base point stays the same), with Black's materials when its template has a renderer with any, otherwise White's. A set whose sides differ only in colour gives Black only materials; one look for both sides gives Black nothing. The promotion templates fall back the same way.
- **The world's sets** are listed on the arbiter (`ChessArbiter.pieceSets`, index 0 the default; [Settings](settings.md#the-arbiters-piece-sets-step-136)), and each board syncs which of them White and Black wear (`ChessBoard.whitePieceSet` / `blackPieceSet`, indexes, 0 after a reset; [Networking](networking.md#synced-fields-per-board)). Set by `SetPieceSet(color, index)` / `SetPieceSetAs(name, organizer, color, index)` (checked by `CanSetPieceSetAs`: White or Black, an index in the arbiter's list, and `_MayOwnAs`, so the loader's lock and organizers apply as they do there), told as `ChangePieceSet`, which neither abandons an import nor clears a promotion waiting for its piece; read by `PieceSetIndex(color)`. A join puts the joiner's preferred set on the board (below). No UI of ours changes a board's sets: a world creator who wants players to choose gives them their own UI ([Customizing](customizing.md#chess-piece-sets-steps-123-1210-and-136)).
- **Joining with a preferred set:** `JoinAs(color, name, preferredSet)` puts `preferredSet` (an index in the arbiter's list; `-1` or one outside it for none) on both sides when the other seat is empty (a reset board, the lobby or a loaded board), otherwise on the joined colour alone, also when the joiner holds the other seat, in the same change as the join (`ChangeJoined`), whoever may set sets otherwise. The preference lives on the joiner's client, so the local forms (`Join(color)`, `JoinWhite()`, `JoinBlack()`) pass the arbiter's `preferredPieceSet`; `JoinAs(color, name)` passes `-1`. A refused join, or one of a seat the joiner already holds, changes nothing.
- **Which set, per side** (`CurrentPieceSet(color)`): the arbiter's local override for that colour if set (`PieceSetOverride(color)`, step 13.6), otherwise the set at the board's synced index for it, or the first set for an index this client's list doesn't have (it shouldn't happen, every client running the same world; logged once per colour as a warning). `_AppliedPieceSet(color)` gives the set a side wears now. The view holds no synced fields and no sets of its own (12.10's `whitePieceSet` / `blackPieceSet` and `SetChessPieces` are gone).
- **Applying** (`ApplyPieceSet(color)`, for both sides on Start, and for a side whose current set isn't the applied one: on each board change, `OnBoardChanged`, which covers `ChangePieceSet`, joins, resets and updates from other clients; on each `_Refresh`, which also catches a change no listener was told of, such as a test's `_ClearState`; and on an override change): the side's 6 templates are copied into the view at `_TemplateIndex(color, type)` (`setMeshes`, `setMaterials`, `setOffsets`, `setRotations`, `setScales`, and `setBases`, the square's centre in the piece's own space), each through Black's fallback (`CopyTemplate`), then each of that side's objects takes its template at once and is put back on its place (a travelling one lands, a held one stays in the hand); the other side's are untouched. Its 4 promotion templates are copied the same way (`ghostMeshes`, `ghostMaterials`, `ghostOffsets`, `ghostRotations`, `ghostScales`, at `_GhostIndex(color, ghost)`), and promotion ghosts showing for that side are dressed again at once. On every placing (`PrepareObject`) an object whose type changed (a promotion, a remapping) takes its new template's mesh, materials, scale and fitted `BoxCollider` (the mesh's bounds, in the piece's own space), and every object is turned as its template is, which also straightens a piece a hand has turned. `_ObjectPlace` adds the template's offset, on a square and on a captured place alike. Everything of a side follows its set this way: on the board, in a hand, travelling, captured, promoted and a fallen king (12.8, its tip applied over the template's turn).
- **Drops** are read from the piece's base (`_PieceBase(obj)`: `setBases` through the piece's transform, so it follows a turned piece), not its origin, so a model whose origin is off its base drops where it stands.
- **Noticing the arbiter's override** (decided in 12.3 for the defaults): each view adds itself to its arbiter's piece set listeners on Start (`ChessArbiter._AddPieceSetListener`, once however often it's called; `_RemovePieceSetListener` takes one off), and `SetPieceSetOverride(color, index)` calls each listener's `_OnPieceSetOverrideChanged(color)` when that override actually changes, which applies that side's current set at once if it's another. A call on each change costs nothing between changes, while a cross-behaviour read every frame on every view adds up. A view with no set for a side (an empty list or entry) logs an error once for that side and leaves its pieces as built.
- **Builder:** the default set, **Chess Pieces**, is a prefab (`Prefabs/Chess Pieces.prefab`, step 14.1) nested in the Chess Arbiter prefab and listed alone in its `pieceSets` ([Prefabs](prefabs.md)). **Create Board View** and **Tools > Chess Board > Update Arbiter Prefabs** make both prefabs when missing and otherwise update them in place (`EnsureArbiterPrefabs`), and move the scene's arbiter onto the nested set (`EnsureDefaultPieceSet`); an arbiter not from the prefab with an empty `pieceSets` gets an instance of Chess Pieces as a child, at index 0. The set is laid out in rows of slots (`BuildPieceSet`), each slot holding a one-square **Guide** (the **Piece Set Guide** mesh and material in `Models/`) and the template **Model**: **White Pawn** … **White King** with the generated meshes and the White Pieces material; behind them **White Promotion Knight** … **White Promotion Queen** (`AddPromotionSlots`, step 12.11), each behind its type's slot, with that type's mesh and template transform and the see-through green **Promotion Ghost** material (Standard, Fade, made in `Models/` when missing) on every slot of the mesh; and behind those **Black Pawn** … **Black King**, whose models hold only a renderer with the Black Pieces material and no mesh, so Black uses the fallback (and shows it working). It has no Black promotion slots, so Black's ghosts are White's. A listed set lacking White promotion slots gets them the same way. Step 12.10's **White Pieces** and **Black Pieces**, and a **Chess Pieces** built under the arbiter before 14.1 (an object the prefab doesn't have), are removed when found under an arbiter with the nested set, which takes their place in its `pieceSets`.
- Decided in 12.3: each template stands in a slot of its own, with its own guide, rather than all under the set with one guide, so they can be seen side by side; the template's transform is read relative to its parent, whatever that is.
- Decided in 13.6: a set holds both colours, with Black's falling back on White's, instead of 12.10's one side per set with Black's always turned: a world offers a design as one entry of the arbiter's list, a design whose Black pieces differ in shape (not just colour) is set up as it should look, and a design that differs only in colour costs six renderers.
- Decided in 13.6: who may change a board's sets follows `_MayOwnAs`, since a set change is sent like any other change of the board; a join applies the preference regardless, as part of the join.

### Captured-piece places (step 12.4)

- **Places:** `whiteCapturedPlaces` and `blackCapturedPlaces`, transforms placed by the world creator, each colour's in order. A captured piece stands on its place as on a square (its template's offset and turn, in the view's space); the place's own rotation isn't read. Places are read through their world position (`_CapturedPlacePosition`), so they may stand anywhere, at any height, inside the view or not. Empty entries are skipped.
- **Order from the history, the same on every client:** on every `_Refresh`, `ChessCaptures._CaptureOrder` (static, in `Rules/ChessCaptures.cs` since step 13.4, read through `ChessBoard.CapturedPieces`, which the board's put-back check also uses; it was the view's own before) works out each colour's captured piece types in place order (`capturedTypes`, `capturedCounts`) from the game's board, side to move and move history, which every client has, never from the view's own history of placements, so a late joiner shows the same places. In order:
  - *Pieces the starting position lacks* (a game from a FEN), short of the standard set (1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights, 8 pawns), in the fixed order queen, rooks, bishops, knights, pawns; each piece beyond a type's standard count (a promotion the FEN doesn't record) counts against the missing pawns. The starting counts are the game's board's with its moves undone (captures given back, promotions turned back into pawns). No history is made up to lead to the position: the missing pieces go straight into places.
  - *The captures of the moves before the ply shown* (the game's ply, or replay's `replayIndex`). Promoted pawns are followed square by square, so a promoted pawn that's captured goes to its place as a pawn; a piece whose promotion the history can't show (an extra queen in the starting FEN) keeps its type.
  - *A custom replay position:* the ply's places, then the pieces of the ply's position missing from the custom one (the free moves took them, kings included), appended in the same fixed order, king last. A type the custom position has more of than the ply's had pieces put back from the places (step 13.4): each such piece is taken out of the last place of its type, the places after it moving up one, so it doesn't stand in both.
- **Putting a captured piece back** (step 13.4): in replay, a captured piece standing in a place it keeps and shown (`CapturedLiftable`) is a pickup for a player who may touch the pieces, and a click target ("Select", "Put back" once selected) for one using clicks. Its pickup goes to `ChessBoard.CapturedPickedUp(color, type)` and its drop to `PlaceCaptured(color, type, square)` (`NoSquare` beside the board, which the board refuses, and it goes back to its place); a selected one is lifted over its place and placed by a click on a square or a piece of the other colour. A lifted one is `heldFrom`/`selectedFrom` `NoSquare` with its place in `heldPlace`/`selectedPlace`, let go (or put back) once it no longer keeps that place, replay is left, or the player may no longer touch the pieces; this is checked before the places are handed out again, so it isn't given another place meanwhile. Accepted, the dropped object is mapped onto the square at once (`PlaceCapturedObject`): the place that empties is the last of its type, which needn't be the object's own, so the mapping would otherwise move another object there.
- **Objects in places** (`objectPlace`, `AssignPlaces`): each object keeps a place the position still has for its type, so places are never reordered; an empty place takes a free object of its colour, preferring one that has just left the board (the piece captured), then one of its type, and wears the place's type. Free objects left over (a FEN's spare objects) are hidden, as are captured pieces beyond the last place with a target (`_IsObjectShown`); they're deactivated and come back when needed. The mapping (`Reconcile`) takes pieces coming back to the board from places that empty rather than from places that stay.
- **Trips:** a captured piece travels to its place as before (waiting for the mover to land), from the board's surface to the place's height: the trip is the straight line between them with the arc over it. A piece coming back (a takeback, a step back in replay) travels back the same way. A hidden object that appears is put in place at once.
- **Builder:** **Create Board View** adds **Captured Places** with 16 empty objects per colour (**White 1** … **Black 16**: enough for every piece a side can lose, a king taken by a free move included), two columns of 8 beside the board on the table's surface, below the board's, on the side of the player who captures them: White's pieces to Black's right (−x) from Black's end, Black's to White's right (+x) from White's end (the user's choice in 12.4; the spares stood the other way round before). Rebuilding keeps the old view's places (its **Captured Places** moves over, and the fields keep pointing at the same objects).
- Decided in 12.4: the order is worked out in the view from the board's public history rather than by a new getter on the board, since the view already reads the board's arrays; it runs on every `_Refresh`, a walk over the history that costs little next to placing the pieces.

### Pickups (step 9.2)

- **Each piece object** also has a kinematic `Rigidbody` (the board puts pieces down, not physics), a `BoxCollider` fitted to its mesh (refitted when the model changes), a `VRCPickup` (theft disallowed) and a `ChessPiece` behaviour (`NoVariableSync`) knowing its view and index. Pieces are on the `Pickup` layer, so a carried piece doesn't push players. The builder adds all of these.
- **Who may touch the pieces** is `ChessBoard.MayTouchPiecesAs(name, organizer)` (`MayTouchPieces()` for the local player, `PlayerMayTouchPieces(player)` for any): anyone on a reset board; in replay whoever may make free moves (seated players and organizers, or anyone while nobody is seated); otherwise (the lobby, a game in progress or ended) the seated players, whichever side is to move. Touching isn't moving: the input methods still decide what a pickup or drop does. Decided in 9.2: unlike the board's ownership rule, an organizer outside replay, a seat whose player is away and an idle board don't let a bystander touch the pieces, since none of their touches would do anything; the organizer's tools are the buttons, and an idle board's is reset.
- **`pickupable`:** the view (`_UpdatePickupable`) makes a piece a pickup for the local player when they use pickups, the board lets them touch the pieces, and the piece stands on a square, or in replay in its captured place (step 13.4, above; outside replay captured pieces never are). It runs after every change and once a second, since whether the local player is an organizer can change without the board changing.
- **`ChessPiece.OnOwnershipRequest`** answers `PlayerMayTouchPieces(requestedOwner)` and logs a refusal (`[ChessPiece] <piece>: ownership refused for <name>`).
- **`inputStyle`** (`InputAuto`, `InputPickups`, `InputClicks`): `_UsesPickups()` is true for Pickups, false for Clicks, and `IsUserInVR()` for Auto. `_CycleInputStyle()` changes it on one client (the debug view's **Input style** button).

### Drops (step 9.3a)

- **Forwarding:** each piece's `ChessPiece` passes its `OnPickup` and `OnDrop` (which fire only on the client holding it) to the view as `_OnPiecePickedUp(index)` and `_OnPieceDropped(index)`. The pickup goes to the board as `PiecePickedUp(square)` for the square the piece stands on; the drop as `PieceDropped(from, to)`, with `to` the square under the piece's base (`_DropSquare`: by where it is over the board, whatever its height; `NoSquare` beside the board, which the board rejects). In replay the board makes both free moves.
- **Held piece:** a pickup the board accepts makes the piece `heldObject` (with `heldFrom`); the view doesn't place it while it's held. A pickup the board refuses (a piece of the side not to move, a player whose turn it isn't) is taken out of the hand at once (`VRCPickup.Drop`), and its drop isn't forwarded. A held piece is also let go when the board no longer shows it on `heldFrom` (another client's change took its square) or the local player may no longer touch the pieces; a drop of a piece no longer held is ignored, and the piece is placed from the board.
- **Snapping back:** the board answers a rejected drop with `ChangeRejected`, and the view places every piece from the board on the next frame as for any change, so a rejected piece goes back where it stood. An accepted drop is a move (or free move), placed the same way.
- **Promotion:** a drop the board answers with `InputPromotionNeeded` leaves the pawn standing on the target square (`promotionObject`, `promotionFrom`, `promotionTo`) until `_ChoosePromotion(pieceType)` plays it (`PromotionChosen`). Any change to the board but a received touch (`ChangeActivity`) drops the waiting promotion, putting the pawn back; so would picking up a piece, which nothing allows meanwhile. Since step 12.11 there's no cancel: `_ChoosePromotion` refuses a type other than knight, bishop, rook or queen and the pawn goes on waiting (`CancelPromotion` was removed). The promotion ghosts (step 12.11, below) call it; the picker of step 9.5a did before.

### Castle drop space (step 9.3b, removed in 9.6)

A king castles by being dropped on its rook, which is never ambiguous (see [Player input](player-input.md)), so the castle drop space built in 9.3b (pads beyond the back rank, shown while a king whose castle was ambiguous was held, with click targets from 9.3c) was removed in 9.6 with the board's `KingDroppedOnCastleSpace` and `IsCastleDropSpaceNeeded`. **Create Board View** no longer builds it, and the table is a little shallower (`TableDepthFactor` 2.25).

### Highlights, and the promotion picker (step 9.5a)

- **Promotion picker** (replaced by the promotion ghosts in step 12.11, below): `promotionPicker`, a small world-space canvas (with `VRCUiShape`, like the button panels) with five buttons in a row, Queen, Rook, Bishop, Knight and Cancel, each calling the view's `PressPromoteQueen` … `PressPromoteCancel`, which go to `_ChoosePromotion` or `CancelPromotion`. `UpdatePromotionPicker` shows it only while a promotion waits (`promotionObject`), on the local player's client alone, upright over the promotion square, `PickerHeight` (2.2) squares up, no further off the board's centre along the files than `PickerMaxOffset` (2) squares so it doesn't hang off the board over the a and h files, and turned to face the promoting side's end of the table. It appears for a drop and for a placing click alike (`WaitForPromotion`), and goes when the promotion is chosen or cancelled, or the board changes (`ClearPromotion`; a received touch doesn't count). While it shows, no piece is a pickup (`_UpdatePickupable`) or a click target (`_UpdateClickables`) for the local player, whatever their input style: in VR a hand near a piece would otherwise grab the piece instead of pointing at the picker's buttons. So the picker is the only way out, by a choice or Cancel.
- **Last-move highlight** (a line since step 12.6): `lastMoveLine`, a flat blue bar from the move's start to its end, and `lastMoveMark`, a small blue diamond (as a legal-target mark on an empty square, `SmallMarkScale`) on its start, placed for `ChessBoard.LastMove()` on every `_Refresh` (in replay too, for the move that led to the ply shown), hidden when there's none (a start, a custom replay position). The line's mesh runs one unit along its +z from its origin: the view stands it on the start, turns it towards the end and stretches its z to the distance (its width is the mesh's). The squares (`_LastMoveSquare(move, which)`) are where the piece came from and where it stands now: a castle shows the king's path to its destination, or its rook's path (from its square to the f or d file) when the king doesn't move (Chess960); a castle moving neither shows only the mark. The line lies just above the squares, the mark above it, and the legal-target marks above both.
- **Check lines** (step 12.7): `checkLines`, red bars like the last move's line (placed by the same `_PlaceLine`), one from each piece giving check to the king it attacks in the position shown, on every `_Refresh` (`_UpdateCheckLines`): two for a double check, and the checkmate's line stays once the game ends. Replay shows the checks of the position shown, custom positions included, where both kings can be in check. The checks come from `ChessBoard.Checks(checkers, kings)` (each king on the board shown, the game's or the replay's, and every piece of the other colour attacking it, from `ChessMoves._SquareAttackers`). The builder makes `CheckLineCount` (16, the most pieces that can attack one square); the lines beyond the checks are hidden, and checks beyond the lines aren't shown. They lie just above the last move's line and under its mark.
- **Legal-target marks** (diamonds since step 12.5): `targetHighlights[64]`, one diamond per square (a square turned 45°, lying on it), shown on the held piece's targets (`heldTargets`, worked out on the pickup and again on every `_Refresh`) or the selected one's (`selectedTargets`): the to squares of its legal moves, a castle's rook square and the king's destination. Each target's kind is worked out with them (`heldMarks`, `selectedMarks`): `MarkCapture` on a capture (en passant on the square the pawn moves to), `MarkCastle` on a castle's rook square (castling by dropping the king on its rook), `MarkMove` on the rest (an empty square, a castle's king destination included). `_UpdateTargetHighlights` gives each shown mark its look (`StyleTargetMark`): `targetMaterial` (yellow) scaled to `SmallMarkScale` (half the square's width and height) for `MarkMove`, `targetMaterial` at full size (its corners at the middles of the square's edges) for `MarkCastle`, `captureMaterial` (red) at full size for `MarkCapture`; a view with no material for a kind keeps the mark's own. `_TargetMarkShown` reads the kind back from the look, for tests. It runs on every lift change (`_UpdateLiftedMarks`). None in replay, where every square is a target, and none once the game ends (the board then gives no legal moves).
- **Builder:** **Create Board View** adds them hidden and without colliders (so clicks still reach the pieces and square targets): **Highlights** with **Last Move Line**, **Last Move From**, **Check Lines** (**Check Line 1** to **16**) and **Legal Targets** (a1 to h8, each over its square) (and **Promotion Picker** until step 12.11). Their meshes (**Last Move Line**, **Check Line**, **Last Move Diamond**, **Legal Target Diamond**) and materials (**Last Move Mark**, **Check Line**, and the view's **Target Mark** and **Capture Mark**) are generated in `Models/` when missing.
- Decided in 9.5a (the picker part undone in 12.11): the picker is UI buttons with words, like the board's buttons, rather than piece models to click, so it works the same with a VR pointer and a desktop click and needs no new behaviour; only the local player sees it, since the waiting promotion is local; the legal-target marks were frames rather than dots so a capture's mark wasn't hidden under the captured piece (diamonds since 12.5: a capture's is full size, its corners showing around the piece); replay gets no target marks.

### Promotion ghosts (step 12.11)

- **Ghosts:** `promotionGhosts`, 4 objects under **Promotion Ghosts**, a knight, bishop, rook and queen (`_GhostType(ghost)`, by index), each with a `MeshFilter`, `MeshRenderer`, trigger `BoxCollider` and a `ChessPromotionGhost` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessPromotionGhost.cs`, no synced variables) knowing its view and `pieceType`, whose `Interact` (a VR pointer or a desktop click alike, labelled `Promote to queen` …, a hover text the view sets from the language pack, step 13.5) calls the view's `_ChoosePromotion(pieceType)`.
- **Shown** (`_UpdatePromotionGhosts`, on every `_Refresh`, when a promotion starts or stops waiting and on a change of set) while a promotion waits (`promotionObject`), on the local player's client alone, for a drop and for a placing click alike. Each wears the promoting side's promotion template (`DressGhost`: mesh, materials, size and a collider fitted to the mesh, redone only when the side changes or its set does), turned as the template is (Black's half round, as its pieces are), and floats at `_GhostPosition(ghost)` plus the template's offset: in a row over the promotion square's rank, its bases `GhostHeight` (1.5) squares above the surface, `GhostSpacing` (1) square apart, the row's middle over the promotion square but no further off the board's centre than `GhostMaxOffset` (2) squares along the files, so the row stays over the board by the a and h files; knight, bishop, rook, queen from left to right as the promoting side sees it (along +x for White, −x for Black). `_AreGhostsShown()` says whether all four show, for tests.
- **No cancel:** dropping the pawn on the last rank commits to promoting. `_ChoosePromotion` refuses any other type and the pawn goes on waiting. While the ghosts show, no piece is a pickup or a click target (as with the picker), so a hand near a piece reaches the ghosts. A change to the board (but a received touch) still ends the wait and puts the pawn back (`ClearPromotion`).
- **Builder:** **Create Board View** adds **Promotion Ghosts** (`AddPromotionGhosts`) with the generated meshes and the **Promotion Ghost** material, hidden; the view dresses them when shown. **Promotion Picker** stays among the builder's own children, so rebuilding a view from before 12.11 drops it rather than keeping it as a creator's model.
- Decided in 12.11: the row floats with its bases 1.5 squares up (clear of the pawn, about 0.75 squares tall, and of the tallest piece that can stand under the row, the king, about 1.4 with its cross) and 1 square apart (the placeholder pieces are about 0.7 squares wide), its middle clamped like the picker's (`PickerMaxOffset` 2). The ghosts have colliders fitted to their meshes like the pieces', refitted on a change of set, and triggers so nobody walks into them. The ghosts are in the piece set (a second row of templates) rather than on the view, so each side's can follow its pieces' design.

### Walking through the board (step 9.5a2)

- **Nothing solid to walk into:** the builder gives the table and the board no colliders; the square targets' colliders are triggers (`Interact` still reaches them); each UI canvas (the button panels, and the promotion picker before 12.11) has a trigger `BoxCollider` the size of the canvas, which `VRCUiShape` uses instead of adding a solid one (the pointer still reaches the buttons). The pieces' colliders are unchanged: they're on the `Pickup` layer, which doesn't collide with players. The highlights have no colliders, and the promotion ghosts' are triggers (step 12.11).
- **The blocker:** `blocker`, a solid `BoxCollider` on **Player Blocker** filling the table from the floor to its top, just under the board, so it never stands between a player's eyes or pointer and the board's surface, pieces or buttons. `_UpdateBlocker` (on every `_Refresh`) enables it unless the local player has a seat (`ChessBoard.IsPlayer`). A player collides only on their own client, so each client's blocker decides for its own player: the seated players walk into the table, everyone else is stopped at its edge.
- Decided in 9.5a2: "part of the game" is having a seat, from joining until the board is reset (a lobby, a game, an ended game); organizers without a seat and bystanders are kept out, on a reset board too.

### Move animations (step 9.5b)

- **What travels:** on each `_Refresh` after one or more board changes, every object whose place changed travels there over `moveSeconds` (0.35 s) instead of jumping: eased along the straight line and lifted in an arc over it, `MoveLift` (1) square high for a trip of two squares or more and less for shorter ones, so a castling rook clears the king. The mover and anything else landing on the board start at once; objects leaving it (a capture, en passant's pawn included) wait until those have landed. Each travels from where it stands, so a desktop player's lifted piece and a promotion's pawn (put back when a change drops the promotion) all travel from there.
- **When:** only if every change since the last `_Refresh` may animate (`_ChangeAnimates`: anything but a reset, a load, entering, leaving or continuing replay) and at most `MaxAnimatedObjects` (4) objects changed squares (a castle or a capture is two; a received update holding a few plies stays under it, while stepping to the start of a game in replay doesn't), counted against where each was last placed. Otherwise every object is put in place at once, as before.
- **Snapping:** a piece the local player has just let go of (`_OnPieceDropped`, or let go by the view) is put in place at once, since it's already where it landed, and a rejected drop goes straight back, as in 9.3a. Picking a piece up stops its travel. A later `_Refresh` with the same place for a travelling object lets it carry on; a new place starts a new trip from where it is.
- **Fields:** `animateMoves` (Inspector; off puts everything in place at once, which the earlier `ViewTest` frames use, since they check positions straight after each change), `moveSeconds`, and per object `animating`, `animationFrom`, `animationTo`, `animationStart` (later than now while it waits), stepped in `Update` (`StepAnimations`) while any travels.
- **The checkmated or resigning king falls over** (step 12.8): while the position shown is the end of a game lost by checkmate or resignation (`ChessBoard.FallenKingSquare()`: a game ended that way, shown at its last ply, in the game or in replay, a loaded game included; never a custom replay position, which has no side to move), the loser's king lies on the board: the mated one, or the resigning side's. A draw or a time forfeit lays no king down. It's tipped a quarter turn about its base, which stays over its square (so `_DropSquare(_PieceBase)` still finds it), towards the board's centre along the files or the ranks, whichever it stands further from it along (the ranks when as far: `_FallDirection`), and lifted so its lowest point, by its mesh's bounds as its template turns and sizes it, is as high as when it stands (`FallLift`), so it rolls over the edge of its base rather than sinking into the board. `_ObjectPlace` and `_ObjectRotation` include the fall, so a falling king always stands where `_ObjectPlace` says.
  - **When:** `UpdateFalls`, on every `_Refresh` before placing, sets each object's `fallTarget` (1 for the fallen king, 0 for the rest); `StepFalls` moves `fallAmount` towards it over `fallSeconds` (0.6 s), the angle eased in (amount squared) as a falling piece speeds up. A king falls once the pieces travelling in that placing have landed (`moveSeconds` on: the mating piece), at once when none travels (a resignation), and stands up at once when that end is no longer shown (a step back in replay, a free move, a takeback). A placing that doesn't animate (a reset, a load, entering or leaving replay, `animateMoves` off) lays it down or stands it up at once, as does its leaving its square.
  - Decided in 12.8: the fall is along an axis rather than straight at the centre, so the mesh's bounds give the lowest point exactly for a round piece (diagonally, the bounds' corners would leave it floating); towards the centre so it lies on the board, never off its edge.
- Decided in 9.5b: every client animates every move, its own included (a click placement, the captured piece of a drop, a castle's rook), except the dropped piece itself; a reset or a load never animates, even when it moves only one piece.

### Status display (step 9.5c; on `ChessBoardUI` since 12.1)

- **Objects:** `statusPlayerLabels` and `statusLabels`, two UI texts per panel at the same index. The **Chess Board UI** prefab (step 14.4; built by **Create Board View** before) has a panel (a world-space canvas, leaning back like the button panels, with no collider or `VRCUiShape`, since nothing on it is clicked) at each end of the table, between the board and that end's buttons, which move out to the table's edge; the table is deeper again (2.4 board widths). The players' column is on the left, the status on the right; both shrink their text to fit (TextMeshPro's auto size, down to 8; best fit before 12.17), so long names don't overflow.
- **Players** (`_PlayersTextAt(now)`, a line per seat from `_PlayerTextAt(color, now)`): `White: Ana`, `(open)` for an empty seat, with the side's remaining time (`_RemainingTimeAt`) during and after a game with a clock: `White: Ana  4:58`. `_ClockDisplay(ms)` writes `M:SS`, `H:MM:SS` from an hour, and tenths under ten seconds (`0:09.8`). The lobby and a loaded game show no times (the time control is on the status line, or the Clock button).
- **Status** (`_StatusTextAt(now)`), a line each: a reset board's `Join White or Black to play`; the lobby's `Lobby: Standard, 5+3` (`_ModeText`, `_ClockText`); a loaded game's `Loaded game: <White> vs <Black>` from its tags (`?` for none), with ` (Chess960)` for that mode; in a game, `White to move` (`, in check` when `IsInCheck`), or `White's time is up` once the flag has fallen and the owner hasn't ended the game yet; the result of an ended or loaded game (`_EndingText`); `Black offers a draw`; `White asks to take back to here` (the request is always for the ply shown); and in replay the position (`_ReplayText`): `Replay: 2. Nf3 (3 of 4)` (numbered as the PGN numbers it, `?` while its notation is missing), `Replay: start (0 of 4)`, or `Replay: custom position` after free moves.
- **Results** (`_EndingText`), always with the PGN result: `White wins by checkmate (1-0)`, `… by resignation`, `… on time`, `… by capturing the king`; `Draw by stalemate`, `by agreement`, `by threefold repetition`, `by fivefold repetition`, `by the fifty-move rule`, `by the seventy-five-move rule`, `Draw: insufficient material`, `Draw: Black ran out of time, White can't checkmate` (the drawn time forfeit: the flagged side is the one that was to move); a loaded game's result tag as `White wins (1-0)` or `Draw (1/2-1/2)`, and `Unfinished game (*)` for one without.
- **Updating:** `_UpdateStatus` on every `_Refresh` and `OnNotationFilled` (a replay line's move may have been missing), then every `StatusInterval` (0.1 s) while a clock runs and once a second otherwise, so the side to move's clock counts down without the board changing. Each text is set only when it changes.
- Decided in 9.5c: the same text on both panels, White's line first; the panels sit between the board and the buttons so the buttons stay nearest the player's hand; the lobby shows the mode and clock to everyone (the Mode and Clock buttons are only for the seated players).

### Sound effects (step 12.12)

- **Who plays what:** the view plays only the move sounds, from `audioSource` (a spatial `AudioSource` on **Sounds**, at the board's centre just above the surface): `moveSound`, `captureSound`, `promoteSound`, `checkSound`. `ChessBoardUI` plays every other sound (see [The board's UI](player-input.md#sound-effects-step-1212)), so a world replacing the UI removes them with it. Neither knows the other.
- **Which sound:** a move (`ChangeMove`), a replay step (`ChangeReplayStep`, either way and jumps) or a free move (`ChangeFreeMove`) plays the sound the position shown would play if its last move had just been made, one sound, the most specific (`_ChangeSound`): `checkSound` when a king is in check there (a capture or promotion that checks too, and a checkmate), then `promoteSound` for a promotion, `captureSound` for a capture (en passant included), `moveSound`. The start of a game (no last move) plays nothing. A free move captured when the position has fewer pieces than at the last `_Refresh`. Nothing else plays a view sound (a takeback, a reset, a load, entering or leaving replay).
- **When:** the change is noted in `OnBoardChanged` (`_NoteSound`) and played on the `_Refresh` after it, every client alike, only when it was the only change since the last `_Refresh` (a received update is one change, however many plies it holds; a test makes thousands in a frame), and only when it's heard (`_SoundAllowed`): not in the first `StartupSilence` (1) seconds after the view starts (the board's own start tells a reset), and not a late joiner's first update. The first change seen after the silence settles which: made on this client, or received, which is the catch-up when nothing came first and is silent.
- **Promotion:** a pawn landing on the last rank (`WaitForPromotion`, a drop or a placing click) plays its move or capture sound at once on the promoting player's client; choosing the piece is a `ChangeMove` that plays `promoteSound` (or `checkSound`) everywhere. Other clients get the whole promotion as one change and hear only that.
- **Tests:** `muted` (not saved) stops the playing but not `lastSound` and `soundsPlayed`, which follow what would have played.
- **Builder:** **Create Board View** adds **Sounds** with its `AudioSource` (spatial, logarithmic from 1 m to 15 m, no Doppler, not playing on awake) and the clips from `Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/Sounds/` (`move`, `capture`, `promote`, `move-check`), warning for a missing one.
- Decided in 12.12: the sound is chosen on the `_Refresh` after a change, from the position then, rather than in `OnBoardChanged`, so only one is ever played per frame; a burst of several changes plays none, which keeps tests and a reset's steps quiet without a flag on the board. A late joiner is told apart by its first received update rather than by time, since a catch-up can take a while to arrive; a client in the instance before the board was ever sent loses at most the sound of the first change it receives, which is always a join or a load in practice.
