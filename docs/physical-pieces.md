# Physical pieces

- **Other players see a piece while it's being carried.**
- **32 piece objects per board**, one per piece. A side never has more than 16 pieces, since promotion turns a pawn into another piece rather than adding one. Each object swaps its mesh to match the piece the board says it is, so promotion needs no spare queens. Captured pieces are hidden or set beside the board.
- **Carrying is synced with `VRCObjectSync`** on each piece. It handles ownership on grab, interpolation and late joiners. This is presentation-only networking, owned by the view; the board's synced fields stay the single source of truth for the game.
- **At rest, positions come from the board.** After every `OnBoardChanged()`, every client puts each piece on the square the board says. The player who dropped the piece (its sync owner) computes the same position, so all clients agree. This assumes `VRCObjectSync` doesn't fight a non-owner's local snap. If testing shows it does, the pieces don't use `VRCObjectSync`.
- **Grabbing is gated by the board.** Grabbing a piece takes ownership of the *piece*, which the board's ownership rules don't cover. So the view sets each piece's `VRCPickup.pickupable` for the local player from a board getter ("may the local player touch pieces?"), updated on every change. Bystanders can't grab pieces during a game, but anyone can on a reset board. The pieces' `OnOwnershipRequest` asks the board too, as a second check. Only VR players get pickups; desktop and mobile players select and place pieces by clicking (see [Desktop mode](player-input.md#desktop-mode)).
- **Mapping:** the view keeps an array from square to piece object, rebuilt from board state. A move updates it; a reset or load rebuilds it.

## Implementation

`ChessBoardView` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessBoardView.cs`, step 9.1) is a `ChessBoardListener` holding no game state. Its local space is the board's: the playing surface at y = 0, files a–h along +x, ranks 1–8 along +z (White at −z), the centre at the origin, `squareSize` (0.05) per square.

- **Objects:** `pieces` and `pieceFilters`, 32 each: 0–15 White's, 16–31 Black's, so an object's colour is fixed by its index and only its mesh changes, from `meshes` (indexed by `ChessRules` piece type). Black's face −z, for the knights.
- **Mapping:** `squareObject[64]` (`NoObject` = −1) and `objectSquare[32]` (`NoSquare` for an object off the board), with `objectType[32]` the type each object stands for.
- **A move one ply on** from the position the mapping shows (`ChangeMove` outside replay, or `ChangeReplayStep` forward by one) is applied to the mapping inside `OnBoardChanged`, so the object that moved is the one that was on its from square: the mover to its square (a promotion changes its type), a capture (en passant included) off the board, castling's king and rook to g/c and f/d, both leaving before either lands (Chess960).
- **Everything else rebuilds** from the board: objects standing on a square that now holds something else leave it, then each square without its object takes a free one of its colour, preferring one already of the piece's type, then the one that stood nearest. A position with more than 16 pieces of a colour (only a hand-made one) leaves the extra ones unshown, with a warning.
- **Placing** happens on the frame after a change (`Refresh`, public for tests), like the debug view's drawing, so a test making thousands of changes in a frame stays within Udon's time limit. Objects off the board stand in two columns beside it: White's to White's right (+x) from White's end, Black's to Black's right (−x).
- **Builder:** **Tools > Chess Board > Create Board View** (`Editor/ChessBoardViewBuilder.cs`) builds the view for the selected board (or the scene's only one): a table, the board, the squares and the 32 pieces, replacing an earlier view in place. The piece meshes and materials are generated placeholders in `Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/Models/`, made only when missing; replace them there, or assign other meshes in the view's **Meshes** (origin at the piece's base centre, knights facing +z).

### Pickups (step 9.2)

- **Each piece object** also has a kinematic `Rigidbody` (the board puts pieces down, not physics), a `BoxCollider` fitted to its mesh (refitted when the mesh changes), a `VRCPickup` (theft disallowed), a `VRCObjectSync`, and a `ChessPiece` behaviour (`NoVariableSync`) knowing its view and index. Pieces are on the `Pickup` layer, so a carried piece doesn't push players. The builder adds all of these.
- **Who may touch the pieces** is `ChessBoard.MayTouchPiecesAs(name, organizer)` (`MayTouchPieces()` for the local player, `PlayerMayTouchPieces(player)` for any): anyone on a reset board; in replay whoever may make free moves (seated players and organizers, or anyone while nobody is seated); otherwise (the lobby, a game in progress or ended) the seated players, whichever side is to move. Touching isn't moving: the input methods still decide what a pickup or drop does. Decided in 9.2: unlike the board's ownership rule, an organizer outside replay, a seat whose player is away and an idle board don't let a bystander touch the pieces, since none of their touches would do anything; the organizer's tools are the buttons, and an idle board's is reset.
- **`pickupable`:** the view (`UpdatePickupable`) makes a piece a pickup for the local player when they use pickups, the board lets them touch the pieces, and the piece stands on a square (captured pieces never are). It runs after every change and once a second, since whether the local player is an organizer can change without the board changing.
- **`ChessPiece.OnOwnershipRequest`** answers `PlayerMayTouchPieces(requestedOwner)` and logs a refusal (`[ChessPiece] <piece>: ownership refused for <name>`).
- **`inputStyle`** (`InputAuto`, `InputPickups`, `InputClicks`): `UsesPickups()` is true for Pickups, false for Clicks, and `IsUserInVR()` for Auto. `CycleInputStyle()` changes it on one client (the debug view's **Input style** button).

### Drops (step 9.3a)

- **Forwarding:** each piece's `ChessPiece` passes its `OnPickup` and `OnDrop` (which fire only on the client holding it) to the view as `OnPiecePickedUp(index)` and `OnPieceDropped(index)`. The pickup goes to the board as `PiecePickedUp(square)` for the square the piece stands on; the drop as `PieceDropped(from, to)`, with `to` the square under the piece's base (`DropSquare`: by where it is over the board, whatever its height; `NoSquare` beside the board, which the board rejects). In replay the board makes both free moves.
- **Held piece:** a pickup the board accepts makes the piece `heldObject` (with `heldFrom`); the view doesn't place it while it's held. A pickup the board refuses (a piece of the side not to move, a player whose turn it isn't) is taken out of the hand at once (`VRCPickup.Drop`), and its drop isn't forwarded. A held piece is also let go when the board no longer shows it on `heldFrom` (another client's change took its square) or the local player may no longer touch the pieces; a drop of a piece no longer held is ignored, and the piece is placed from the board.
- **Snapping back:** the board answers a rejected drop with `ChangeRejected`, and the view places every piece from the board on the next frame as for any change, so a rejected piece goes back where it stood. An accepted drop is a move (or free move), placed the same way.
- **Promotion:** a drop the board answers with `InputPromotionNeeded` leaves the pawn standing on the target square (`promotionObject`, `promotionFrom`, `promotionTo`) until `ChoosePromotion(pieceType)` plays it (`PromotionChosen`) or `CancelPromotion()` puts it back (a rejected `PromotionChosen` with `NoPieceType`). Any change to the board but a received touch (`ChangeActivity`), and picking up any piece, drops the waiting promotion. The picker that calls these comes in step 9.5a.

### Castle drop space (step 9.3b)

- **Objects:** `castleSpaces[2]`, by side (`ChessBoard.CastleKingSide`, `CastleQueenSide`): pads the size of a square, as thick as the board and just above its surface, with no collider. The builder makes them hidden (with a generated **Castle Space** material) and makes the table deep enough for them.
- **Where:** the square off the board beyond the king's back rank, next to its castling destination (g or c): for White beyond g1/c1 (rank "0"), for Black beyond g8/c8 (`CastleSpacePosition(color, side)`). Off the board, so a drop there is no square, and away from the spare pieces beside the board.
- **When:** `UpdateCastleSpaces` shows a side's space (`castleSpaceShown`) while the local player holds a king (`heldObject`, a pickup the board accepted, so the side to move's) and `ChessBoard.IsCastleDropSpaceNeeded(side)`; it runs on every pickup, drop, let-go and `Refresh`, so a change while the king is held (the game ending, replay) hides it. Standard castles and Chess960 castles moving the king two or more squares never show one; in replay none is shown.
- **Dropping:** a held king let go over a shown space (`CastleSpaceUnder`, by where it is over the pad whatever its height) goes to the board as `KingDroppedOnCastleSpace(side)` instead of a drop; anywhere else as before. A drop where no space is shown is beside the board, which the board rejects.
- Desktop mode (step 9.3c) shows the same spaces while a king is selected.
