# 2D board view

A flat board on a world-space canvas showing one board's position at a time, with left / right buttons to choose which board (step 12.13), and a replay of its own (step 12.14) with a move list beside the board (step 12.15). In a holder, players can move and resize it (step 12.16). It's for watching: spectators across the room, a wall showing every game in turn, or a fixed view beside each table. Nothing on it acts on a board: the selector, the step buttons and the move list are its only inputs, and they change only the view.

## What it shows

- **A position of the board's game** (step 12.14; 12.13 showed what the board showed, replay included): the latest unless the view's own step buttons have moved it, White at the bottom, each piece as a sprite. The board's replay doesn't move it, and a custom replay position is never shown: the view shows the game's positions only. The last move into the position shown has its two squares tinted, and a king in check in it is red.
- **Which board:** `Board N`, numbered from 1 as the settings menu numbers them (`boardNumber + 1`).
- **Its players and status lines** as the board's UI writes them (the status display, [Physical pieces](physical-pieces.md#status-display-step-95c-on-chessboardui-since-121)): the seats with their clocks (`GG` after a flag fall, step 12.9), whose turn, check, the result, a draw offer, a loaded game's names. The lines about the board's replay position are left out: a takeback request reads `White asks for a takeback` (the UI's "to here" is the board's replay position), and the replay line is the view's own, `Replay: 2. Nf3 (3 of 4)` for the position it shows when that isn't the latest.

## Its own replay (step 12.14)

- **Step buttons** along the bottom, `<<` (the start), `<` (one move back), `>` (one move forward), `>>` (the latest). Always shown, and greyed and not clickable where they'd do nothing: `<<` and `<` at the start, `>` and `>>` on the latest.
- **Following the game:** on the latest position, a move moves the view on to the new latest; anywhere earlier, it stays. A takeback or continuation cutting the history below the ply shown (or changing the moves under it), a reset and a load bring it to the latest. A takeback above the ply shown leaves it where it is.
- **Switching board** (the selector, or a synced switch arriving) starts on the new board's latest position.
- **Synced:** with **Synced** on, the ply shown is synced with the board chosen, so everyone sees the same position; off, each player steps by themselves.

## Move list (step 12.15)

- **Where:** right of the board, running the full height of the view; the selector row, the texts and the step buttons end at its left edge. The canvas grew to make room (decided in 12.15): the board keeps its 100-pixel squares and the view is 1.36 m wide instead of 0.88 m.
- **Columns:** a row per move number (`1.`, `2.`, … from the game's starting move number), White's move on the left and Black's on the right. A game starting with Black to move (from a FEN) starts with an empty White cell. No rows before the first move.
- **Current move:** the move into the position shown (the view's ply, 12.14) has a yellow cell with black text; none at the start.
- **Clicking a move** shows the position after it (`ShowPly`, so synced as the step buttons are). Empty cells aren't clickable.
- **Scrolling:** when the rows don't fit, a scrollbar shows on the right, and dragging or the mouse wheel over the list scrolls it. Whenever the ply shown changes (the step buttons, a click, following the game on the latest, switching board, a received ply), the list scrolls just enough to bring the current move's row into view (to the top at the start); otherwise a player's own scrolling stays. Each turn of the mouse wheel moves the list one row, whatever the input's delta (step 13.9, as on the history wall).
- **Notation:** the board's SAN (`notation`). A move whose SAN isn't filled in yet (a loaded game, a late joiner) shows an empty cell until `OnNotationFilled` brings it.

## Moving and resizing (step 12.16)

A view in a **holder** can be moved and resized by players; a view without one stays where it's placed, as in 12.13 to 12.15, and needs nothing new. The view itself knows nothing of the holder.

- **Moving:** a grey bar under the view (the **Grip**, centred under its bottom edge) is a `VRCPickup`: grab it and the view follows, put anywhere and left where it's let go (a kinematic rigidbody without gravity). In VR the view turns and tilts as the hand turns the bar. After a desktop player picks the bar up, the view and the bar stand upright, turning only with the bar's heading, while held and after letting go, until a VR player picks it up (for everyone when synced, late joiners included). (At first it was kept upright for everyone; the client runs of 12.16 changed it at the user's request.)
- **Resizing:** a small cube just outside the view's bottom right (the **Corner**) is a second pickup: holding it sizes the view, keeping its aspect, by how far along the view's bottom edge it is held, between `minSize` and `maxSize` (0.25 and 3 times the size it has in the scene; 0.5 before the client run). The view grows and shrinks about the middle of its bottom edge, which keeps its place above the bar, so a small view doesn't sink onto the bar. On letting go the corner goes back beside the view's corner.
- **Reach in VR:** a VR hand must be within `vrReach` (0.05 m) of the bar or the corner to grab it (the pickups' `proximity`, set on `Start` for a VR player), so reaching for the view's buttons doesn't grab it. Desktop players keep the pickups' own reach.
- **`movable`** (Inspector, on the holder, on by default): off, neither pickup can be picked up (`pickupable` false) and the bar and corner are hidden. `SetMovable(bool)` changes it at run time.
- **Synced** (the view's own tick): on, the grip has a `VRCObjectSync`, so everyone sees the view move smoothly while it's held and a late joiner sees it where it was left (picking it up takes ownership, as any pickup), and the size is a synced field of the holder's. Off, there's no `VRCObjectSync` and nothing is sent: each player's moves and sizes stay their own.
- **Putting it back:** `PutBack()` returns it to where it was placed in the scene, at its size there (synced, for everyone). Nothing calls it on its own and no button is built for it: a world can call it from a button of its own.

## Implementation of the holder (step 12.16)

`ChessBoard2DViewHolder` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessBoard2DViewHolder.cs`), on the holder object, whose children are the view, **Grip** and **Corner**.

- **Where the pickup is** (decided in 12.16): a bar of its own under the view, not the canvas: a pickup's colliders would take the clicks meant for the buttons and the move list (on desktop, a click would grab the view), and `VRCObjectSync` forces continuous sync on behaviours on its own object, so the view's manual-synced board and ply, and the holder's size, are on other objects. The view isn't the grip's child: the holder places it (`_Follow`) in `PostLateUpdate` whenever the grip has moved (`hasChanged`) or the size changed, from the layout on `Start` (so a world creator may lay them out differently in the editor): the middle of the view's bottom edge (from its `RectTransform`) keeps its place from the grip, and the view's place from it and its bottom right corner are scaled by the size; the corner keeps its place from that corner unless it's held here.
- **Resizing** (decided in 12.16): a second pickup at a corner rather than buttons. `_SizeFor(local, baseRight, min, max)` (static) is the corner's place (from the bottom edge's middle, less its own offset from the view's corner, in the grip's space) projected on the bottom right corner's place at size 1. While held here the size is sent at most every `SendInterval` (0.2 s) and once on letting go; a received size (`syncedSize`) is eased to (`_TargetSize()`). The corner has no `VRCObjectSync`: every client puts it at the view's corner from the size.
- **Turning:** an upright flag (`SetUpright`, `IsUpright()`; synced as `syncedUpright`, sent with the size) set on picking up the grip: on for a desktop player, off for a VR player. Off, the view takes the grip's whole rotation; on, only its heading (`_Upright(rotation, fallback)`, static: the last heading when it points straight up or down), and the grip's bar (`gripModel`) is turned upright on it. The grip itself isn't turned: VRChat places a held pickup and `VRCObjectSync` sends that place, so turning the grip upright (tried first) was undone on letting go and never reached the others. (Decided first to keep the view upright for everyone, then to tilt it for everyone, then this, at the user's request in the client runs.)
- **Held here** is read from the pickups each frame (`IsHeld`, `currentPlayer.isLocal`), so the grip and corner need no behaviour of their own (one on the grip would be forced to continuous sync).
- **The `VRCObjectSync`** (decided in 12.16): the prefab's grip has none, and a build callback (`Editor/ChessBoard2DViewSyncCheck.cs`) adds or removes it before every VRChat build to match the view's **Synced**, so ticking it in the Inspector is all a world does. **Toggle 2D View Synced** matches it too.
- **For world scripts:** `SetSize(value)`, `Size()`, `PutBack()`, `SetMovable(value)`; for tests also `_Follow()` and `_TargetSize()`. Each let-go and received size is logged: `[ChessBoard2DViewHolder] <holder>: moved to (x, y, z) here[, sent]`, `resized to 1.5 here[, sent]`, `resized to 1.5 by <owner>`, `put back here[, sent]`.
- **Prefab:** the **Movable 2D Chess Board View** prefab (step 14.8, [Prefabs](prefabs.md#movable-2d-chess-board-view)) is the holder at the origin with a nested **2D Chess Board View** as its view, a grip (a bar 4 cm under the view, 0.5 m wide at most and narrower to stay 2 cm clear of the corner at `minSize`: 0.3 m on the prefab's view) and a corner (an 8 cm cube), both on the Pickup layer with trigger colliders (players walk through them) and the **2D View Handle** material (`Models/`). The bar is fitted to the default `minSize`: after changing **Min Size**, check the bar against the corner at that size. Its grip has no `VRCObjectSync` (its view isn't synced); the build callback adds one to an instance's grip as an added component (decided in 14.8: enough, no variant per case). Until step 14.12 the builders made it (**Create Movable 2D Board View**).

## Locked to one board (step 12.14)

`lockedBoard` (Inspector): when set, the view shows only that board, its left / right buttons are hidden and disabled (`Board N` still shown), and stepping between boards is refused. For a world with a fixed view beside each table. A locked view doesn't read its `boards` (decided in 12.14); the wiring script leaves it empty, and fills it once `lockedBoard` is cleared (on the next save, Play Mode or build), giving a working selector.

## Choosing the board

- **`boards`:** set in the Inspector, in the order the buttons step through (as the settings menu's). The [wiring script](prefabs.md#wiring) fills an empty one with every board in the scene by board number (not for a locked view).
- **Stepping:** left and right step to the previous or next board that isn't reset (`LifecycleReset`), wrapping round and skipping reset ones. The board shown isn't left when it's reset. With no other board in use, both buttons do nothing. It starts on the first board.
- **Synced** (Inspector tick): on, the board chosen is a synced field and a press takes ownership of the view, so everyone sees the same board, late joiners included. Off, each player chooses for themselves and nothing is sent.

## Implementation

`ChessBoard2DView` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessBoard2DView.cs`), a `ChessBoardListener`.

- **Listening, not polling** (decided in 12.13): the view listens to the board it shows only, moving its listener (`RemoveListener` on the old board, `AddListener` on the new) when it steps, so boards nobody looks at don't tell it of their changes. Other boards' lifecycles are read only when a button is pressed. It draws on the frame after a change (`_Refresh`, public for tests), as the other views do, and brings its texts up to date every `ChessBoardUI.StatusInterval` while a clock runs and once a second otherwise, since the clocks run without the board changing. `OnNotationFilled` redraws the texts (the replay line may name a move it didn't know).
- **The texts** come from static forms of the UI's status methods, taking the board (`ChessBoardUI._PlayersTextFor`, `_PlayerTextFor`, `_StatusTextFor`, `_EndingTextFor`, `_ReplayTextFor`), so the two never differ. The UI's instance methods (`_PlayersTextAt` and the rest) forward to them. No getter had to be added to `ChessBoard`: `PieceAt`, `LastMove`, `Checks` and the public state were enough. Since 12.14 the view writes `ChessBoard2DView._StatusTextFor(board, now, ply)`: the UI's `_GameStatusTextFor` (its `_StatusTextFor` without the takeback request and the replay line, which `_StatusTextFor` adds to it), the request as `asks for a takeback`, and `ChessBoardUI._PlyTextFor(board, ply)` (the replay line for any ply, which `_ReplayTextFor` uses too) when the ply isn't the latest.
- **Its own position** (12.14): `viewPly` (0 the start, the board's `moveCount` the latest) and `onLatest`. On every `_Refresh` the view builds the position itself (`_BuildPosition`, static): the game's `board` and `state` copied onto its scratch arrays and the moves from `viewPly` on undone with `ChessPosition.UnmakeMove`, as `StepTo` builds the board's replay board. It never reads `PieceAt`, so the board's replay can't reach it. The last-move tint is `moveHistory[viewPly - 1]`, the checks come from `ChessMoves._SquareAttackers` on the scratch position. Decided in 12.14: the position is rebuilt from the latest on every redraw rather than kept and moved a move at a time, so a takeback, a load or a received update can't leave it out of step; in the test, `<<` on a 300-ply game takes a few milliseconds (see the PLAN's run).
- **Following** (`FollowGame`, on every `_Refresh`): off the latest, the view remembers the move that led to its ply (`shownMove`); when the history no longer reaches the ply, has another move there, or a reset or load was among the changes (`OnBoardChanged` reads the board's `lastChange`), or the ply is the latest again (a takeback to exactly it), it goes back on the latest. `ShowPly(ply)` (public; `CanShowPly` says whether it would change anything) is what the step buttons' `PressFirst`, `PressBack`, `PressForward` and `PressLast` call, and the move list's cells do (12.15). `ViewPly()`, `IsOnLatest()`, `_ShownPieceAt(square)` and `_IsStepEnabled(index)` are for tests.
- **Move list** (12.15): a pool of rows, not one element per move (decided in 12.15): `moveNumbers` (one per pooled row) and `moveCells`, `moveCellImages`, `moveCellLabels` (two per row, White's at `2 * row`, Black's at `2 * row + 1`), enough rows to fill the list's height and one more (28 of 50 pixels in the built view), so a game of any length needs no more objects. The list is a `ScrollRect` (`moveList`) whose `moveListContent` is an empty rect the view sizes to every row; scrolling moves it, its `onValueChanged` calls `_OnMoveListScrolled`, and `DrawMoveList` fills the pool with the rows the scroll position puts in view, moving `moveListRows` up by the part of a row scrolled past (the viewport, `moveListViewport`, has a `RectMask2D`). The wheel moves the list one row a notch (step 13.9, as the history wall's `_OnScrolled`, 13.8): the scroll rect moves the content by the wheel's delta times `scrollSensitivity` (one row), and that delta is about 120 a notch through the Input System (ClientSim) and about 1 through the legacy input, so `_OnMoveListScrolled`, when the wheel turned that frame (`Input.GetAxisRaw("Mouse ScrollWheel")`), replaces the scroll rect's move with one `scrollSensitivity` step in its direction from where the list last was, clamped to `_MoveListMaxScroll()`, and sets the scroll rect's `verticalScrollbar` to match (`SetValueWithoutNotify`: the scroll rect has already set it from its own move, which would show for a frame). A drag is left alone. The view's own scrolling (`SetScroll`, used by `UpdateMoveList` and `_ScrollMoveList`) updates where the list last was too, so a wheel turn in the same frame as a step button or a received move adds a step to the view's scroll instead of undoing it. The rows sit in the viewport, so a drag or the wheel over a cell reaches the scroll rect. Each cell is a button with a `ChessMoveListCell` (`view`, `cell`, `_Press` calling `_PressMoveCell(cell)`, as the settings menu's buttons), since a UI event carries no arguments. `_Refresh` scrolls (`UpdateMoveList`) only when the board or ply shown differs from the last it scrolled for, then draws; `OnNotationFilled` draws. For tests: `_MoveCellPly(cell)`, `_MoveCellOf(ply)`, `_IsMoveInView(ply)`, `_IsMoveCellCurrent(cell)`, `_MoveListScroll()`, `_MoveListMaxScroll()`, `_ScrollMoveList(offset)` (as a player scrolling). Colours: `moveCellColor` (clear), `currentMoveColor` (yellow), `moveTextColor`, `currentMoveTextColor`. Drawing the list adds about 3 ms to a step on a 300-ply game in ClientSim.
- **Text** (12.17): every text is TextMeshPro (`TextMeshProUGUI`; UI `Text` before): `boardLabel`, `playersLabel`, `statusLabel`, `stepLabels`, `moveNumbers` and `moveCellLabels`, at the sizes, places and colours they had. The font is TMP's default ([Customizing](customizing.md#text-and-font-step-1217)).
- **Step buttons:** `stepButtons` and `stepLabels` (`StepFirst` … `StepLast`), `interactable` off where they'd do nothing, their labels in `stepLabelDisabledColor` then (the button's own tint greys its image).
- **Locking:** `lockedBoard`; `IsLocked()`, and `SetLockedBoard(board)` to change it at run time (null unlocks it). Locked, `ShownBoard()` is the locked board, `ShownIndex()` is −1, `Show(index)` shows the locked board whatever the index, `_Step` does nothing, and `leftButton` / `rightButton` are deactivated and not interactable.
- **Stepping:** `PressLeft` / `PressRight` call `_Step(direction)`, which asks `_StepTarget(inUse, current, direction)`, a static method over which boards are in use, so the rule is checked with any number of boards. `Show(index)` switches board (public for tests); `ShownIndex()` and `ShownBoard()` say which is shown.
- **Sync of the ply** (12.14): a second synced field, `syncedPly`, `LatestPly` (−1) for the latest, so clients following the game stay on it whatever moves reach them first. A step (`ShowPly`) takes ownership and sends both fields; a board switch sends `LatestPly`. On `OnDeserialization` the view shows the ply sent. A ply the game on this client doesn't reach beyond yet (the view's update can arrive before the board's: on a late joiner it did, in the 12.14 run) is kept waiting (`waitingPly`) while the view shows the latest, and shown once the board's moves go past it, logged as `showing ply N of M (received earlier, the game here now reaches it)`; a step here, a board switch, a reset or a load drops it. `_ReceivePly(ply)` takes a ply as `OnDeserialization` does, for tests. When the owner's view goes back to the latest by itself (a takeback below it, a reset), the owner sends `LatestPly`, so a late joiner isn't left on a ply the others have left. Each step is logged as `[ChessBoard2DView] <view>: showing ply N of M[, the latest] (chosen here[, sent] / chosen by <owner>)`.
- **Sync:** manual sync with one synced field, `syncedIndex` (12.13; `syncedPly` since 12.14). With **Synced** on, a step takes ownership of the view (`Networking.SetOwner`; anyone may, there's no `OnOwnershipRequest`), sets the field and calls `RequestSerialization`; `OnDeserialization` shows the board it names. With it off the field is never sent and arriving data is ignored. Each switch is logged (`[ChessBoard2DView] <view>: showing Board N (<why>)`: on start, chosen here, chosen here and sent, or chosen by the owner) for comparing clients' logs. A late joiner starts on the first board and switches once the synced field arrives (about 12 s after joining in the 12.13 run). See [Networking](networking.md) and [Ownership](ownership.md).
- **Sprites:** `whiteSprites` and `blackSprites`, 6 each, pawn to king. The defaults are the PNGs in `Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/Sprites2D/` (`PawnW.png` … `KingB.png`); swap them in the Inspector for another set. Each square has a background `Image` (`squareImages`) and a piece `Image` (`pieceImages`, aspect kept, disabled on an empty square). The square colours (`lightSquare`, `darkSquare`, `lastMoveTint`, `checkTint`) are Inspector fields.
- **Prefab:** the **2D Chess Board View** prefab (step 14.8, [Prefabs](prefabs.md#2d-chess-board-view); no boards, not locked or synced, its origin the canvas's centre) is the canvas (with `VRCUiShape` and a trigger collider over it, so the pointer hits it and players walk through it): the selector row (`<`, `Board N`, `>`), the 8×8 board of 100-pixel squares, the players and status texts under it, the step buttons along the bottom (12.14) and the move list on the right with its scrollbar (12.15), at 0.001 scale (1.36 m wide since 12.15, 0.88 m before). A world places it where spectators stand (the test scene's stands 2.5 m behind the boards, facing them), or one locked to a board behind that board. The test scene has a second view, synced, beside the unsynced one the tests use. Until step 14.12 the builders made it (**Create 2D Board View**, **Create Locked 2D Board View**, **Create Another 2D Board View**).
- **The debug view** uses the same sprites since 12.13, in place of its FEN letters (`pieceImages`, `whiteSprites`, `blackSprites`, set by its builder from the same folder).
