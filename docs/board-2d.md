# 2D board view

A flat board on a world-space canvas showing one board's position at a time, with left / right buttons to choose which board (step 12.13), and a replay of its own (step 12.14). It's for watching: spectators across the room, a wall showing every game in turn, or a fixed view beside each table. Nothing on it acts on a board: the selector and the step buttons are its only inputs, and both change only the view.

## What it shows

- **A position of the board's game** (step 12.14; 12.13 showed what the board showed, replay included): the latest unless the view's own step buttons have moved it, White at the bottom, each piece as a sprite. The board's replay doesn't move it, and a custom replay position is never shown: the view shows the game's positions only. The last move into the position shown has its two squares tinted, and a king in check in it is red.
- **Which board:** `Board N`, numbered from 1 as the settings menu numbers them (`boardNumber + 1`).
- **Its players and status lines** as the board's UI writes them (the status display, [Physical pieces](physical-pieces.md#status-display-step-95c-on-chessboardui-since-121)): the seats with their clocks (`GG` after a flag fall, step 12.9), whose turn, check, the result, a draw offer, a loaded game's names. The lines about the board's replay position are left out: a takeback request reads `White asks for a takeback` (the UI's "to here" is the board's replay position), and the replay line is the view's own, `Replay: 2. Nf3 (3 of 4)` for the position it shows when that isn't the latest.

## Its own replay (step 12.14)

- **Step buttons** along the bottom, `<<` (the start), `<` (one move back), `>` (one move forward), `>>` (the latest). Always shown, and greyed and not clickable where they'd do nothing: `<<` and `<` at the start, `>` and `>>` on the latest.
- **Following the game:** on the latest position, a move moves the view on to the new latest; anywhere earlier, it stays. A takeback or continuation cutting the history below the ply shown (or changing the moves under it), a reset and a load bring it to the latest. A takeback above the ply shown leaves it where it is.
- **Switching board** (the selector, or a synced switch arriving) starts on the new board's latest position.
- **Synced:** with **Synced** on, the ply shown is synced with the board chosen, so everyone sees the same position; off, each player steps by themselves.

## Locked to one board (step 12.14)

`lockedBoard` (Inspector): when set, the view shows only that board, its left / right buttons are hidden and disabled (`Board N` still shown), and stepping between boards is refused. For a world with a fixed view beside each table. A locked view doesn't read its `boards` (decided in 12.14); the builder still fills it, so clearing `lockedBoard` gives a working selector.

## Choosing the board

- **`boards`:** set in the Inspector, in the order the buttons step through (as the settings menu's). The builder fills it with every board in the scene by board number.
- **Stepping:** left and right step to the previous or next board that isn't reset (`LifecycleReset`), wrapping round and skipping reset ones. The board shown isn't left when it's reset. With no other board in use, both buttons do nothing. It starts on the first board.
- **Synced** (Inspector tick): on, the board chosen is a synced field and a press takes ownership of the view, so everyone sees the same board, late joiners included. Off, each player chooses for themselves and nothing is sent.

## Implementation

`ChessBoard2DView` (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/ChessBoard2DView.cs`), a `ChessBoardListener`.

- **Listening, not polling** (decided in 12.13): the view listens to the board it shows only, moving its listener (`RemoveListener` on the old board, `AddListener` on the new) when it steps, so boards nobody looks at don't tell it of their changes. Other boards' lifecycles are read only when a button is pressed. It draws on the frame after a change (`Refresh`, public for tests), as the other views do, and brings its texts up to date every `ChessBoardUI.StatusInterval` while a clock runs and once a second otherwise, since the clocks run without the board changing. `OnNotationFilled` redraws the texts (the replay line may name a move it didn't know).
- **The texts** come from static forms of the UI's status methods, taking the board (`ChessBoardUI.PlayersTextFor`, `PlayerTextFor`, `StatusTextFor`, `EndingTextFor`, `ReplayTextFor`), so the two never differ. The UI's instance methods (`PlayersTextAt` and the rest) forward to them. No getter had to be added to `ChessBoard`: `PieceAt`, `LastMove`, `Checks` and the public state were enough. Since 12.14 the view writes `ChessBoard2DView.StatusTextFor(board, now, ply)`: the UI's `GameStatusTextFor` (its `StatusTextFor` without the takeback request and the replay line, which `StatusTextFor` adds to it), the request as `asks for a takeback`, and `ChessBoardUI.PlyTextFor(board, ply)` (the replay line for any ply, which `ReplayTextFor` uses too) when the ply isn't the latest.
- **Its own position** (12.14): `viewPly` (0 the start, the board's `moveCount` the latest) and `onLatest`. On every `Refresh` the view builds the position itself (`BuildPosition`, static): the game's `board` and `state` copied onto its scratch arrays and the moves from `viewPly` on undone with `ChessPosition.UnmakeMove`, as `StepTo` builds the board's replay board. It never reads `PieceAt`, so the board's replay can't reach it. The last-move tint is `moveHistory[viewPly - 1]`, the checks come from `ChessMoves.SquareAttackers` on the scratch position. Decided in 12.14: the position is rebuilt from the latest on every redraw rather than kept and moved a move at a time, so a takeback, a load or a received update can't leave it out of step; in the test, `<<` on a 300-ply game takes a few milliseconds (see the PLAN's run).
- **Following** (`FollowGame`, on every `Refresh`): off the latest, the view remembers the move that led to its ply (`shownMove`); when the history no longer reaches the ply, has another move there, or a reset or load was among the changes (`OnBoardChanged` reads the board's `lastChange`), or the ply is the latest again (a takeback to exactly it), it goes back on the latest. `ShowPly(ply)` (public; `CanShowPly` says whether it would change anything) is what the step buttons' `PressFirst`, `PressBack`, `PressForward` and `PressLast` call, and 12.15's move list will. `ViewPly()`, `IsOnLatest()`, `ShownPieceAt(square)` and `IsStepEnabled(index)` are for tests.
- **Step buttons:** `stepButtons` and `stepLabels` (`StepFirst` … `StepLast`), `interactable` off where they'd do nothing, their labels in `stepLabelDisabledColor` then (the button's own tint greys its image).
- **Locking:** `lockedBoard`; `IsLocked()`, and `SetLockedBoard(board)` to change it at run time (null unlocks it). Locked, `ShownBoard()` is the locked board, `ShownIndex()` is −1, `Show(index)` shows the locked board whatever the index, `Step` does nothing, and `leftButton` / `rightButton` are deactivated and not interactable.
- **Stepping:** `PressLeft` / `PressRight` call `Step(direction)`, which asks `StepTarget(inUse, current, direction)`, a static method over which boards are in use, so the rule is checked with any number of boards. `Show(index)` switches board (public for tests); `ShownIndex()` and `ShownBoard()` say which is shown.
- **Sync of the ply** (12.14): a second synced field, `syncedPly`, `LatestPly` (−1) for the latest, so clients following the game stay on it whatever moves reach them first. A step (`ShowPly`) takes ownership and sends both fields; a board switch sends `LatestPly`. On `OnDeserialization` the view shows the ply sent. A ply the game on this client doesn't reach beyond yet (the view's update can arrive before the board's: on a late joiner it did, in the 12.14 run) is kept waiting (`waitingPly`) while the view shows the latest, and shown once the board's moves go past it, logged as `showing ply N of M (received earlier, the game here now reaches it)`; a step here, a board switch, a reset or a load drops it. `ReceivePly(ply)` takes a ply as `OnDeserialization` does, for tests. When the owner's view goes back to the latest by itself (a takeback below it, a reset), the owner sends `LatestPly`, so a late joiner isn't left on a ply the others have left. Each step is logged as `[ChessBoard2DView] <view>: showing ply N of M[, the latest] (chosen here[, sent] / chosen by <owner>)`.
- **Sync:** manual sync with one synced field, `syncedIndex` (12.13; `syncedPly` since 12.14). With **Synced** on, a step takes ownership of the view (`Networking.SetOwner`; anyone may, there's no `OnOwnershipRequest`), sets the field and calls `RequestSerialization`; `OnDeserialization` shows the board it names. With it off the field is never sent and arriving data is ignored. Each switch is logged (`[ChessBoard2DView] <view>: showing Board N (<why>)`: on start, chosen here, chosen here and sent, or chosen by the owner) for comparing clients' logs. A late joiner starts on the first board and switches once the synced field arrives (about 12 s after joining in the 12.13 run). See [Networking](networking.md) and [Ownership](ownership.md).
- **Sprites:** `whiteSprites` and `blackSprites`, 6 each, pawn to king. The defaults are the PNGs in `Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/Sprites2D/` (`PawnW.png` … `KingB.png`); swap them in the Inspector for another set. Each square has a background `Image` (`squareImages`) and a piece `Image` (`pieceImages`, aspect kept, disabled on an empty square). The square colours (`lightSquare`, `darkSquare`, `lastMoveTint`, `checkTint`) are Inspector fields.
- **Builder:** **Tools > Chess Board > Create 2D Board View** (`Editor/ChessBoard2DViewBuilder.cs`) builds the canvas (with `VRCUiShape` and a trigger collider over it, so the pointer hits it and players walk through it): the selector row (`<`, `Board N`, `>`), the 8×8 board of 100-pixel squares, the players and status texts under it, and the step buttons along the bottom (12.14), at 0.001 scale (0.88 m wide). It sets the sprites' import type to Sprite when it isn't. A new view stands 2.5 m behind the boards (along +z), facing them; rebuilding replaces the selected view, or the scene's only one, where it stands, keeping its `boards`, `lockedBoard` and **Synced**. **Tools > Chess Board > Create Locked 2D Board View** (12.14) builds a view locked to the selected board, 1.2 m behind it, or replaces the one already locked to it where it stands. **Create Another 2D Board View** (12.14) always adds a view, `ChessBoard2DView (N)`, beside the others; the test scene has a second view this way, synced, beside the unsynced one the tests use.
- **The debug view** uses the same sprites since 12.13, in place of its FEN letters (`pieceImages`, `whiteSprites`, `blackSprites`, set by its builder from the same folder through `ChessBoard2DViewBuilder.LoadPieceSprites`).
