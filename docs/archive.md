# Previous games: the archives, shown on the history walls

Two kinds of archive (step 13.10), both a `GameArchive` ([below](#two-kinds-step-1310)): the **instance's** (`InstanceGameArchive`, `GameArchive` before 13.10), every game played in the instance, synced, on the **Previous games** wall; and the **personal** one (`PersonalGameArchive`), the local player's own games, kept in their VRChat PlayerData across visits and instances, on the **My games** wall ([Personal archive](#personal-archive-step-1310)). Most of this page is the instance's.

- The instance's archive stores the games played in this instance: every game that gets logged (finished, or unfinished when reset or continued from an earlier position).
- Per game: game ID, continued-from reference, mode, time control, players, ending and winner, starting FEN, the packed move history and the per-move clock history.
- **No notation stored.** A game loaded from the archive starts with every notation entry empty, and the board's fill-in code ([Networking](networking.md#filling-in-missing-notation), time-budgeted) works it out over the next frames on every client: the loading player's and each one receiving the load. (Decided in 10.2b, replacing the loading player computing it and syncing it with the load: the fill-in already runs on every client, and nothing more is sent.)
- **Storage:** Udon can't sync arrays of arrays, so games are stored flattened: one `int[]` with every game's moves back to back, plus per-game arrays of IDs, names and numbers.
- **Capacity: 50 games**, in 10 **holders** of 5 games each. It's a circular log: once full, each new game overwrites the oldest. Reading the archive gives the games in chronological order (oldest first) whichever holder slot each sits in.
- **Bandwidth is the real constraint.** A manual-sync serialization sends *all* of a behaviour's synced fields, so with one behaviour holding every game, each game added would resend the whole archive. Split into holders, adding a game reserializes only the holder it goes into: 5 games of 80 plies are about 3 KB of moves and clocks. Late joiners still receive every holder, which is VRChat's job.

## One writer

- **One controller, `InstanceGameArchive`, and its holders, `GameArchiveHolder`.** Every board on every client hands each game it logs to its own client's archives, the instance's among them (each client logs each game once, on the transition; see [Game endings](game-endings.md)).
- **Only the archive's owner writes to the holders.** The archive object's owner is the instance's writer: when it's handed a game it isn't already holding (by game ID), it puts it in the next slot, takes ownership of that holder and serializes it. Every other client's copy of the same game is ignored, so the archive never gets two writers racing for a holder.
- **A holder may only be owned by the archive's owner:** its ownership request handler refuses anyone else, on every client. Nobody asks for the archive object itself (it refuses every request); VRChat hands it on when its owner leaves.
- **The recent games:** every client also keeps the last 5 games it logged in a local-only holder of its own, which never syncs. When a client becomes the archive's owner (the previous owner left, perhaps just as a game ended and before it was archived), it adds every recent game that isn't in the archive and that it has never seen in the archive. The same check runs whenever the owner is handed a game, and whenever a holder update arrives, so nothing is lost while the holders are still arriving. A client remembers which of its recent games it has seen archived (on its own client or in a holder update): once full, the archive overwrites its oldest games, and a recent game that was archived and then overwritten mustn't come back as the newest when that client becomes the owner. (Found in 10.3: after filling the archive, a client's overwritten recent games counted as waiting again.)
- **Readiness:** the owner only writes once every holder holds the instance's data: it has received the holder's state (late joiners get it on joining), or it has owned the holder since its start as the master (the instance's first player). Until then games wait in the recent holder.
- **Handover (step 10.1b):** VRChat hands a leaving player's objects to one remaining player, so the archive and the holders its owner held pass on together. The new owner archives its recent games that aren't in the archive (on `OnOwnershipTransferred`), taking ownership of each holder it writes, which the holders allow since it now owns the archive. A game the old owner archived reaches everyone before its leaving does; one it hadn't archived (it ended as the owner left) was logged by every client that had the game, the new owner included, and is in its recent games. A holder update can still arrive after the new owner wrote: if its write was refused, the arriving update replaces the holder, and the game, missing from it again, is archived again by the flush that every update triggers.

## Loading a game

- **Loading a game onto a board:** a player picks a game on a history wall (either one). A "Load it here" button then appears on every board currently in its reset state, visible to that player; pressing one loads the game onto that board, which enters replay mode with it. The player who loaded it keeps the board until they join a seat (step 13.2, [Ownership](ownership.md)). No parsing is involved when the archive has the game's moves (the instance's always, the personal one for the games handed in this session): the packed moves are copied straight from the archive. A personal game saved in an earlier session is imported from its saved PGN instead. The players and result are shown the same way as for an imported PGN, and continuing from a position works the same way, including the `ContinuedFrom` reference. Since 13.10 the game keeps the ending it was logged with (a resignation shows as one, not just as `Black wins`).
- **A game can only be loaded onto a board in its reset state.** A board with a game in progress refuses. So does a board showing another loaded or imported game; it has to be reset first.

## Implementation

`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/InstanceGameArchive.cs` (`GameArchive.cs` before 13.10) and `GameArchiveHolder.cs` (step 10.1a). Its log lines keep their `[GameArchive]` tag.

- **`GameArchiveHolder`** (manual sync): `Capacity` (5) slots. Each slot has a `sequences` entry (the order it was archived in, from 1; `0` for an empty slot, so the zeroed arrays are an empty holder), `gameIds`, `continuedFroms`, `whites`, `blacks`, `startFens`, and `InfoLength` ints in `infos` (mode, ending, winner, base time, increment, move count); `moves` and `clocks` hold the slots' moves back to back, in slot order. `_WriteSlot` replaces one slot (rebuilding the flattened arrays), `_SlotOf(gameId)` finds one, `_MoveOffset`, `_CopyMoves`, `_CopyClocks` read one. As on the board, the state is packed into separate synced fields (`_PackState`, from `OnPreSerialization`, writing `""` for a `null` string) and unpacked (`_ReceiveState`, from `OnDeserialization`; an update that isn't valid is ignored), so a `localOnly` holder (the recent games) neither sends nor takes anything. `_Send()` takes ownership and requests serialization (`sends` counts them, for tests). `OnOwnershipRequest` allows only the archive's owner, never for a local-only holder, and logs each refusal (`[GameArchiveHolder] Holder 0: ownership refused for <name>`); a `_Send` that finds the holder still someone else's logs a warning and sends nothing. `ready` is set on the first update received, or on `Start` when the local player is the master and owns it (a late joiner can be reported as the owner of everything before the owners are known).
- **`InstanceGameArchive`** (manual sync, no synced fields: its ownership marks the writer): `holders` (10) and `recent` (the local-only holder). `_AddGame(gameId, continuedFrom, white, black, mode, ending, winner, startFen, baseTime, increment, moves, clocks, count, pgn)` (the logged PGN, ignored here: the archive keeps no notation) puts the game in `recent` (unless it's there already; a game with no ID or no moves is refused) and then calls `_Flush()`, which, on the archive's owner with every holder ready, archives each recent game, oldest first, that no holder has and this client hasn't seen archived (`recentSeen`, per recent slot: set when a holder has the game, checked at the start of every `_Flush` on every client, when this client archives it, or when it's stored already archived; cleared with the archive). `_IsRecentWaiting(slot)` says whether a recent game still waits (the debug view's `WAITING`). Archiving gives a game the next sequence number (one more than the highest in the holders) and puts it in slot `sequence % Capacity` across the holders (holder `slot / 5`, entry `slot % 5`), so the 51st game overwrites the 1st. `OnOwnershipTransferred` to the local player and each holder update call `_Flush()` too. Each game archived is logged (`[GameArchive] archived game <sequence>, <ID> (<White> vs <Black>), in Holder <h> slot <s>`), and so is becoming the owner (`[GameArchive] GameArchive now owned here: N recent game(s) added`), so only the owner's log has archived lines. `_StoreRecent` is `_AddGame` without the flush, for tests, and `held` (step 10.1b, for the debug view's **Hold archive**) stops this client archiving anything, as an owner that left before archiving.
- **Reading:** `GameCount()`, and per chronological index (0 the oldest): `GameIdAt`, `ContinuedFromAt`, `WhiteAt`, `BlackAt`, `StartFenAt`, `ModeAt`, `EndingAt`, `WinnerAt`, `BaseTimeAt`, `IncrementAt`, `MoveCountAt`, `_SequenceAt`, `CopyMovesAt`, `CopyClocksAt`, `HasMovesAt` (always true here), `PgnAt` (`""`: no notation kept). The order is the holders' slots sorted by sequence, rebuilt after any change. Every change (a game archived, a holder update, clearing) goes through the base's `_Changed()` ([below](#two-kinds-step-1310)), which counts it in `changes` and tells the walls. Nobody may delete from it (`CanDelete()` and `DeleteGame` are `false`).
- **Boards:** the arbiter's `archives` (since 13.10; `ChessBoard.archive` before); `LogGame` hands the game to each archive there with the board's ID, `ContinuedFrom`, seats, mode, the ending and winner logged, starting FEN, time control, moves, clocks and the PGN it logged. A board without an arbiter archives nothing.
- **Scene:** the **Game Archive** prefab (step 14.5, [Prefabs](prefabs.md#game-archive)) is the archive with its 10 holders and the recent holder as children. **Tools > Chess Board > Create Game Archive** places an instance of it and adds it to the arbiter's `archives`; running it again replaces the archive in place, pointing whatever pointed at the old one at the new one. **Update Archive Prefab** saves the prefab again and makes a scene's archive built before 14.5 an instance of it.

## Two kinds (step 13.10)

Udon has no interfaces ([Udon constraints](udon-constraints.md)), so **`GameArchive`** (`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/GameArchive.cs`) is an abstract behaviour with virtual methods, as `ChessBoardListener` is, and the two kinds derive from it. A field typed `GameArchive` (the arbiter's `archives`, a wall's `archive`, the pick) reaches either kind's overrides.

- **Readers** (index 0 the oldest): `Capacity()`, `GameCount()`, `IndexOf(gameId)`, `GameIdAt`, `ContinuedFromAt`, `WhiteAt`, `BlackAt`, `StartFenAt`, `ModeAt`, `EndingAt`, `WinnerAt`, `BaseTimeAt`, `IncrementAt`, `MoveCountAt`, `_SequenceAt` (0 on an archive without sequence numbers), `HasMovesAt(index)` (whether `CopyMovesAt` and `CopyClocksAt` can give the game's moves and clocks), `CopyMovesAt`, `CopyClocksAt`, `PgnAt(index)` (the game's PGN as logged, when the archive keeps it; `""` otherwise) and `_LoadPgnAt(index)` (the text a board imports when `HasMovesAt` is false: the personal archive's saved copy with its tags).
- **Changing:** `_AddGame(…, pgn)` (each kind keeps what it wants), `CanDelete()` and `DeleteGame(gameId)` (`false` in the base).
- **Shared:** `changes`, the walls registered with it (`walls`, `_AddWall`, each wall adding itself on `Start`), and the arbiter, which introduces itself on its `Start` (`arbiter`, `_SetArbiter`), so neither the archive nor its walls need a field for it. `_Changed()` is every change: it counts it, clears the arbiter's pick when the picked game was this archive's and is gone (deleted, or overwritten as the oldest; no message says why), and calls each wall's `_OnArchiveChanged()`. `_OnArbiterChanged(change)` passes a change of the pick on to the walls (`_OnSelectionChanged()`).
- **The arbiter's list:** `ChessArbiter.archives` (set once per world: the Game Archive and the Personal Game Archive) replaces each board's `archive` field. One world-wide list rather than one per board: a world can no longer send different boards to different archives, which seems rare; a per-board override can come back later as a new field.

## Personal archive (step 13.10)

`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/PersonalGameArchive.cs`: the local player's own games, local only, nothing synced.

- **Which games:** only those with the local player's display name as White or Black (the board identifies players by name); a game without the player's name, or with no ID or no moves, isn't kept.
- **Capacity:** `capacity` (default 20, in the Inspector) slots. A new game goes into an empty slot, or once full over the oldest game; a game it holds already (by ID) is replaced in its slot. A capacity lowered later leaves the slots past it unread; raised, the slots already saved are read again.
- **Order from the game ID:** an ID starts with the game's start time in UTC ([Game IDs](game-ids.md)), so the archive's order (index 0 the oldest) is its games sorted by ID, and the oldest to overwrite is the one with the lowest ID. No sequence numbers are stored (`_SequenceAt` is 0).
- **Saved as PGN** to the local player's PlayerData (VRChat Persistence): one string key per slot (`keyPrefix` + `game` + slot) holding the game's PGN as the board logged it, and one int key (`keyPrefix` + `capacity`). `keyPrefix` defaults to `siggi.chess.personal.`, so a world's own keys don't clash. PGN rather than packed moves (decided 2026-10-26): a standard our own changes can't break, so a later version never needs code to read an older format; export from the personal wall is the stored text; and loading reuses the importer. PlayerData is gzipped and the gzipped size counts toward the limit, which makes PGN about as small as packed moves (a 300-ply game with millisecond clocks: 2.2 KB as PGN, 2.1 KB as packed ints in base64), so 20 games of 300 plies take about 43 KB; measured in the client (13.10), 20 random games of 300 plies with millisecond clocks took 16 KB of the 100 KB limit. Each save asks for the storage used (`Networking.RequestStorageUsageUpdate`; the client reports 0 until asked, found in the 13.10 client check), and `OnPersistenceUsageUpdated` logs it (`GetPlayerDataStorageUsage` of `GetPlayerDataStorageLimit`, kept in `storageUsed`; ClientSim's figures aren't accurate). PlayerData keys can't be deleted, so an empty slot, a deleted game's too, is the empty string.
- **Two tags only in the saved copy** (decided 2026-10-26): the slot's first two lines are `[PlyCount "…"]` (the number of plies, a standard supplemental tag) and `[Ending "…"]` (custom, the ending's stable name, `ChessGame.EndingName`: `resignation`, `agreement`, `checkmate`…; see [Game endings](game-endings.md)), then the PGN exactly as logged. `PgnAt(index)` drops those two lines (no regex: they're always first, in this order), so Export PGN shows the logged PGN; the board's own PGN doesn't change.
- **Reading:** nothing is read until `OnPlayerRestored` for the local player (`_Restore`): then each slot's tags only (`ChessPgn._ReadTags`, the importer's tag regex, never the move text): ID, `ContinuedFrom`, White, Black, the mode (`Variant`), `Ending` and `Result` (ending and winner; without `Ending`, `EndingUnknown` with the result's winner), `FEN`, `TimeControl` and `PlyCount`. A slot without a `GameID` counts as empty and is logged. Until restored `GameCount()` is 0, so the wall shows just its title, and a game handed in waits in memory and is saved once restored.
- **Moves in memory for this session's games:** a game handed in by a board this session keeps its packed moves and clocks beside its PGN (`HasMovesAt` true), so loading it is the instance archive's instant copy. Games read from PlayerData have only their PGN until loaded through the importer. Not cached: the moves of a game imported earlier this session.
- **Deleting:** `CanDelete()` is true; `DeleteGame(gameId)` writes the empty string to its slot.
- **Not secret:** other players can read a player's PlayerData (VRChat's docs), so the history isn't private.
- For tests: `_Forget()` (forgets every game in memory, as before restoring), `_Restore()`, `_ClearSaved()` (writes `""` to every slot), `saves`, `_SlotKey(slot)`, `_SavedText(pgn, plies, ending)`, `_LoggedPgn(text)`.
- **Scene:** **Tools > Chess Board > Create Personal Game Archive** adds one beside the instance's archive and to the arbiter's `archives` (replacing the scene's in place, keeping its capacity and key prefix). Its prefab is step 14.6's.

## The pick, and who tells whom (step 13.10)

- **The pick** is two local fields on the arbiter, `selectedArchive` (`null` for none) and `selectedGameId` (not synced: each player's pick is their own). A wall's entry pressed calls `arbiter._SelectGame(archive, gameId)`: it picks that game, or clears the pick when it's the game already picked; a game in another archive replaces the pick, and the earlier archive's walls stop showing it. `_ClearSelection()`, `HasSelection()`.
- **Told, not polled:** each archive tells its walls of every change (`_OnArchiveChanged`), replacing the wall's `Update` polling of `changes`. The arbiter tells every archive in its list, every board (registered on the board's `Start`, `_AddBoard`) and every settings menu (`_AddSettingsMenu`) of any change (`_OnArbiterChanged(change)`, `ChessArbiter.ChangeSettings` or `ChangeSelection`); each archive tells its walls of a pick (`_OnSelectionChanged`), each board its listeners (`ChessBoardListener.OnArbiterChanged(board, change)`), so the UI shows or hides Load it here at once.

### History wall (step 10.2a)

`Assets/Siggi/ChessBoard/GameHistoryWall.cs`, a view of the archive (no sync, no state of its own). It lists every archived game, newest first (step 13.8), one entry each on two lines: `#<sequence>  <White> vs <Black>`, then `<ending> (<result>), <N> moves`, with `, Chess960` for that mode and `?` for a missing name, e.g. `#12  Ana vs Bo` / `White wins by checkmate (1-0), 12 moves`. The ending's words come from the language pack by `ChessGame.EndingKey(ending, winner)` (`ending_unfinished`, "Unfinished", for an unterminated game; `ending_time_forfeit_draw`, "Draw on time", for a drawn time forfeit; step 13.5), which `ChessBoardUI._EndingText` uses too; the entry's text itself is `wall_row` (`wall_row_chess960`), one string with a line break (`\n`) between the two lines. The title always reads `Previous games` (`wall_title`, the wall's `titleKey`), however many games there are. It redraws when its archive tells it of a change (`_OnArchiveChanged`, step 13.10; polled in `Update` before), so a game archived on this client and a holder update arriving (a late joiner receiving the holders) both show. `_RefreshIfChanged` stays for tests and the first drawing.

- **Order:** the archive's indices stay oldest first (`GameArchive` doesn't change); the wall maps between them. Entry 0, at the top, shows the latest game: entry `e` shows `_RowText(count - 1 - e)`, `_SelectRow(e)` picks that game, and the selection colours the entry of `_SelectedIndex()`. `_RowText(index)` and `_SelectedIndex()` take and return archive indices, so `ChessBoardUI` uses them unchanged. Once a game is archived, every entry moves down one and the new one is at the top; a pick stays on its game (it's kept by ID).
- **Scrolling:** the entries sit in a vertical `ScrollRect` (a viewport with a `RectMask2D`, a scrollbar on the right shown while they don't fit, as the 2D view's move list, 12.15), dragged with the pointer or scrolled with the mouse wheel. Entries for games not in the archive are hidden (their `GameHistoryRow`'s object inactive, their text empty) and the content is `count × entryHeight` tall, so the list scrolls only when the games overflow it, and an empty archive shows just the title. The wall leaves the scroll position alone, except that its first drawing scrolls to the top. Each turn of the mouse wheel moves the list one step (`scrollSensitivity`, an entry): the scroll rect's `onValueChanged` calls `_OnScrolled`, which, when the wheel turned that frame (`Input.GetAxisRaw("Mouse ScrollWheel")`), replaces the scroll rect's move with one step in its direction, clamped to `_MaxScroll()` (from the content's current height, so a wheel turn in the frame the content shrinks, the archive emptied, still lands within the new end), and sets the scrollbar to match (`SetValueWithoutNotify`: the scroll rect has already set it from its own move, which would show for a frame), since the wheel's delta is about 120 a notch through the Input System (ClientSim) and about 1 through the legacy input, and the scroll rect multiplies it by `scrollSensitivity` (step 13.8). A drag is left alone.
- **Fields:** `archive` (either kind, step 13.10), `titleKey`, `title`, `rows` (one TextMeshPro text, `TextMeshProUGUI`, per entry, as many as the archive holds; a UI `Text` before 12.17), `rowTargets` (each entry's `GameHistoryRow`, its object the entry), `deleteButtons` and `deleteLabels` (13.10, the personal wall's), `content` (the scroll view's content), `entryHeight`, `scroll`, `viewport` and `scrollbar` (the scroll view, its viewport and scrollbar, for the wheel's step), `exportButton`, `exportLabel`, `pgnBox`, `pgnHint` and `pgnField` (13.10, Export PGN); `_Refresh`, `_RefreshIfChanged`, `_OnArchiveChanged`, `_OnSelectionChanged`, `_OnScrolled`, `_MaxScroll`, `_TitleText`, `_RowText(index)`, `_DateText(gameId)`, `_EnsureEntries`, `drawnChanges`, and for tests `refreshes` and `selectionDraws` (how often it drew everything, and the pick).
- **Scene:** **Tools > Chess Board > Create History Wall** (`Editor/GameHistoryWallBuilder.cs`) builds a portrait world-space canvas for the scene's only instance archive, 1.2 m wide and 1.6 m tall: the title with the Export PGN button at its right end, then the scroll view with an entry per game the archive holds (50), each 100 canvas pixels (10 cm) tall, and the hidden PGN box over the list's lower half. A new wall goes 2.5 m to the left of the leftmost board (along -x), level with the boards along z and facing them, so it's ahead and to the left from the spawn. Running it again replaces the wall in place (parent, position, rotation, scale). It writes `Previous games` into the title's text in the scene too, so the wall's orientation shows in the editor; at run time the wall writes it from the language pack. **Create Personal History Wall** (13.10) builds the same for the scene's only personal archive (20 entries, each with a delete button at its right end, `titleKey` `wall_title_personal`, `My games`), beside the instance's wall, further left as seen from the boards (a wall's width and 30 cm along its own left, -z), facing the boards the same way.

### The walls since step 13.10

- **Either kind:** `GameHistoryWall` shows either archive through its `archive`. It registers with it on `Start` (`_AddWall`), and the archive tells it of every change and every change of the pick ([above](#the-pick-and-who-tells-whom-step-1310)).
- **Entries match the archive's `Capacity()`:** at start the wall makes the entries it lacks by instantiating its last one (with its `GameHistoryRow`, its `Label` text and its `Delete` button, found by name), so a capacity changed in the Inspector needs no rebuilt wall. Until its archive is restored the personal wall shows just the title.
- **The personal wall's entries** start with the game's date and time from its ID in place of `#<sequence>` (where `_SequenceAt` is 0): `wall_row_personal` (`wall_row_personal_chess960`), e.g. `2026-10-26 21:30  Ana vs Bo`, in the player's local time (`DateTime.ToLocalTime`).
- **The pick** is drawn on the entry of the arbiter's pick when `selectedArchive` is the wall's archive ([Loading a game](#loading-a-game-step-102b)).
- **Export PGN** (`exportButton`, `_PressExport`), shown while the arbiter's pick is in the wall's archive: it opens a PGN box on the wall for the local player (`pgnBox`, a read-only multi-line `TMP_InputField` as the board UI's copying part, with the same hint, `pgn_copy_hint`), over the list's lower half (decided in 13.10: it stays on the wall, and the picked entry is usually near the top), holding the picked game's PGN as loading it onto a board and pressing **PGN** would show it: `ChessPgn.WriteGame`, which `ChessBoard.GamePgn` writes with too, from what the archive holds (the Event and Site tags from the arbiter's first board until 13.11 moves them to the arbiter). Pressed again (`Hide PGN`, `wall_export_hide`), or the pick changed or cleared, the box closes. From the personal archive the PGN is `PgnAt(index)`, at once. The instance's archive keeps no notation, so for its games the wall works the SAN out from the packed moves over the next frames (`_ExportFor`, `ExportBudgetMs`, 10 ms a frame, as the board's notation fill-in), the box saying `Working…` (`wall_pgn_working`) until it's in. `_ExportText()` gives the box's PGN once in, for tests.
- **Delete** (the personal wall's `deleteButtons`, each entry's `Delete` child calling its `GameHistoryRow._PressDelete`, then the wall's `_PressDelete(entry)`), shown only while `archive.CanDelete()` (never on the instance's wall) and the entry has a game: the first press turns it to `Sure?` (`wall_delete_sure`) for 3 seconds (`DeleteArmTime`), a second press within them calls `DeleteGame`.

### Loading a game (step 10.2b)

- **Picking:** each entry of the wall is a UI Button covering both its lines (the canvas has `VRCUiShape` and a trigger collider, like the board's button panels) with a `GameHistoryRow` (`wall`, `index`: the entry's, 0 at the top), whose `_Press` calls the wall's `_SelectRow(index)`. That picks the entry's game on the arbiter (`ChessArbiter._SelectGame(archive, gameId)`, step 13.10; the wall's own `selectedGameId` and `selectionChanges` before), by ID, since a game's entry moves with every game archived, or unpicks it when it's the one picked; an entry with no game does nothing. The pick is the local player's alone (nothing synced), its entry's text drawn in `selectedColor` (the rest in `rowColor`). A picked game deleted or overwritten in its archive is unpicked at once (the archive's `_Changed`). `_SelectedIndex()` gives the pick's index in the wall's archive, -1 when it's none or in the other archive.
- **Load it here:** a board button (`ChessBoardUI.ButtonLoadHere`, `_PressLoadHere`; on `ChessBoardView` before 12.1), on both ends' panels next to the Join buttons, shown while `CanLoadGame()` (a reset board) and the board's arbiter has a pick (`HasSelection()`). So it shows on every reset board, only to the player who picked. The UI brings its buttons up to date when its board passes on the arbiter's change (`OnArbiterChanged`, step 13.10; it polled the wall before, whose `historyWall` field is gone: the UI needs no wall). Pressing it calls `ChessBoard.LoadSelectedGame()` (the local player's form of `LoadSelectedGameAs(name)`, so the presser is recorded as `loadedBy`, step 13.2), which, once the load is accepted or its import started, clears the pick, so the other boards stop offering it. A game it imports (below) shows its progress and failure on the UI as a pasted PGN's does.
- **`ChessBoard.LoadSelectedGame()`** (13.10) finds the archive and the game from the arbiter's two fields: when the archive `HasMovesAt` the game, `LoadArchivedGameFrom(archive, gameId)`; otherwise (a personal game from an earlier session) an import of its `_LoadPgnAt`, the saved copy with its `Ending` tag (`_StartImportAs`, recording the presser as the import's `importBy`), as **Load PGN**'s Import starts one. The loaded board syncs as now, so other players need nothing from the loader's personal archive.
- **`ChessBoard.LoadArchivedGameFrom(source, gameId)`** (13.10, and its `…As` form; `LoadArchivedGame(gameId)` stays and looks the game up in the arbiter's archives, the first holding it with its moves): refused unless `CanLoadGame()` and `source` holds the game with its moves. Copies the game's moves and clocks from the archive and passes them to `_LoadGameEndingAs` with empty notation, its starting FEN, mode, White and Black, its ending as a PGN result (`ChessPgn.ResultText`: `1-0`, `0-1`, `1/2-1/2` or `*`), its ID, time control, and the ending it was logged with, which the loaded game keeps (with the result's winner; an unfinished game loads without an ending, as before). Before 13.10 the board worked the ending out again, as for an import without an `Ending` tag: a checkmate or stalemate from the position, otherwise `EndingUnknown` with the result's winner (an archived resignation showed as `Black wins`). Continuing it gets a `ContinuedFrom` naming it (`_ContinueGameIdAt`).
- **Scene:** **Create History Wall** builds the entry buttons; **Create Board View** adds the button. Replacing a view points the behaviours that referred to the old one (`ViewTest`, debug views, `ArchiveTest`) at the new one, and replacing the wall does the same for the wall.
